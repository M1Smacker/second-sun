    The book¶

    Part 1 · The sky and the data¶

    1. Stars, names and missionsKepler and TESS, how stars are named, and how to read brightness, distance and time.
    2. Light curvesHow a star's brightness graph reveals a planet, and how TESS collects it.
    3. Our dataFrom a NASA camera to your D: drive: what our data is, how big, and how good.

    Part 2 · Two suns¶

    1. Two sunsBinary stars, the planets that orbit both, and where those planets can survive.
    2. Wandering transitsWhy two-sun planets' dips arrive early, late, or not at all, and why that's our chance.
    3. Orbits and the three-body problemKepler's law, the closest safe orbit, and why crossings change time and length.

    Part 3 · Finding dips¶

    1. Noise and statisticsWhy averaging doesn't always help, what SNR means, and how often noise fakes a dip.
    2. Our detectorHow our search finds dips, and six honest tests of how good it is.
    3. Fake planetsHiding planets of known size in real data to measure what the search can see.

    Part 4 · Is it real?¶

    1. VettingEvery impostor that fakes a planet, the test that catches each one, and our strict bar.
    2. Pixels and neighboursChecking a dip is on our star, not a faint neighbour, using the pixels themselves.

    Part 5 · The professionals' tool¶

    1. STANLEYHow guessing whole orbits lets a search stack crossings that don't keep time, and what it said about TOI-1338.

    Part 6 · How the project runs¶

    1. Your workshopWhere everything lives, how a day of work becomes a page, and the safety rules.
    2. Credit and publishingHow a find becomes a named result, and what journals say about AI.
    3. Our mistakes, as lessonsFifteen logged mistakes, five patterns, and the rule each one left.
