# Open questions, wave 1a

Ranked by how much the answer changes the hunt. Owner = which session or wave should pick it up.

1. **Why does TESS have only 2 transiting circumbinary planets** when Kostov et al. 2020 (arXiv:2008.02756) predicted "hundreds" of multi-transit candidates? Search gap (samples, sectors not searched), follow-up bottleneck (candidates exist but unconfirmed), or physics/occurrence (Oddo et al. 2026 upper limit of 33%)? *Owner: main-session gap report, with 1b (searches) and 1c (coverage).* **This is the biggest open question.**
2. **Are there circumbinary candidates already sitting on ExoFOP** (TOIs/CTOIs with CBP comments, e.g. TIC 319011894 from Davies et al. 2026, the Oddo et al. 2026 candidate, Thornton et al. 2025's 27)? A target we "discover" may already be listed. *Owner: 1b / wave 5.*
3. **TIC 172900988 b's period:** 188.8 to 204.1 d (photodynamical, Kostov et al. 2021) vs a 150-d RV planet (Sairam et al. 2023, arXiv:2310.07527). Same planet or a second one? Has TESS caught a later transit that settles it? *Owner: wave 3 (ground-truth set); read Sairam et al. in full.*
4. **TOI-1338 b's transit status in 2025 to 2027.** The discovery paper says ≲30% chance per conjunction after 2025-09-12; NASA's release said transits cease after Nov 2023 and resume ~8 years later. A 2024 photodynamical paper (arXiv:2404.18415) may have updated predictions. Matters for using it as a ground-truth test. *Owner: wave 3.*
5. **Which Kepler circumbinary systems has TESS re-observed, and did any transit?** Needed for the ground-truth set. *Owner: wave 3 / 1c.*
6. **Can a single-transit circumbinary candidate be submitted as a CTOI**, given CTOI forms expect a period? *Owner: wave 5.*
7. **Counting rule for the book:** the archive's `cb_flag` includes BEBOP-4 (AB) b, which its discoverers call a brown dwarf, plus contested post-common-envelope ETV planets, and excludes 2M1510 (AB) b and HW Vir. Proposed rule in report §2.6 (lead with 17 secure planets around normal binaries); needs Ahmed's OK as a book decision.
8. **Is there a written NASA policy on which discoveries get press releases?** My "what it takes" section is inferred from 4 releases + 3 citizen-science features, not from a policy document. *Owner: wave 5.*
9. **"PH1 = Planet Hunters 1"** is my inference; the Yale release says only "dubbed PH1". Low stakes; check Schwamb et al. 2013 full text when writing the precedents chapter.
10. **KIC 3853259 (AB)b**, named by Mogan & Zanazzi 2024 as a wide non-transiting planet around a tight binary: identity and status not checked (Semantic Scholar rate-limited). Relevant to the tight-binary-desert story.
11. **Deeg & Doyle 2024 review** ("The Way To Circumbinary Planets", arXiv:2408.15307) read only at abstract level (no HTML version). It is the newest broad review and a likely beginner key read; read the PDF in a later session.
