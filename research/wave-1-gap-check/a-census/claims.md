## C. Physics primer

| # | Claim | Source | Label | Checked |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Holman & Wiegert (1999) circumbinary stability limit: a_c/a_b = 1.60 + 5.10e − 2.22e² + 4.12μ − 4.27eμ − 5.09μ² + 4.61e²μ², μ = m2/(m1+m2); fit to test-particle survival over 10⁴ binary periods; valid ~0 ≤ e ≤ 0.7, 0.1 ≤ μ ≤ 0.5; unstable islands exist just beyond a_c at n:1 resonances (3:1 to 9:1). | arXiv:astro-ph/9809315 (targeted read of Eq. 3) | VERIFIED | 2026-10-19 |
| C2 | Typical a_c ≈ 2.5 to 3.5 a_bin, i.e. a critical period ≈ 4 to 7 P_bin, for the known transiting systems (my computation: Kepler-38 4.3, Kepler-413 4.1, Kepler-1661 4.5, TOI-1338 4.9, TIC 172900988 6.6 P_bin). | derived from C1 + abstract parameters (script in report appendix) | VERIFIED (derived) | 2026-10-19 |
| C3 | Pile-up: the innermost transiting planets sit at only ~1.3 to 1.6 × the critical period (Kepler-38 1.30, TOI-1338 b 1.34, Kepler-1661 1.39, TIC 172900988 1.45, Kepler-413 1.60); Kepler-1647 b (20.6×) is the outlier. | derived (C1 + abstracts) | VERIFIED (derived) | 2026-10-19 |
| C4 | The pile-up near the stability limit is not explained by observing bias alone (Martin & Triaud 2014); "it is not a selection bias that the innermost planets reside near the stability limit for eight of the nine observed systems" (Li, Holman & Tao 2016). | arXiv:1404.5360; arXiv:1608.01768; Martin 2018 (full) | VERIFIED (Martin 2018 full) / LIKELY (abstracts) | 2026-10-19 |
| C5 | Known transiting CBP periods span 49 to 1108 days; the smallest is ~3 R_E. No CBP smaller than 3 R_E has been found, though <3 R_E planets are >75% of single-star discoveries; this is attributed to detection difficulty, not absence. | Martin 2018 (full); Martin & Fabrycky 2021, arXiv:2101.03186 | VERIFIED / LIKELY | 2026-10-19 |
| C6 | Transit timing variations scale as ~(T_p T_bin²)^(1/3)/(2π) and "may be on the order of days, and hence significantly longer than the transit duration", so phase-folding fails; all Kepler CBP discoveries were made by eye. | Martin 2018 (full), citing Agol et al. 2005; Armstrong et al. 2013 | VERIFIED | 2026-10-19 |
| C7 | Concrete example: TOI-1338 b transit intervals were ≈93 and ≈95 days, durations ~0.3, ~0.4 and ~0.6 d for three transits of the same star. | Kostov et al. 2020 (full) | VERIFIED | 2026-10-19 |
| C8 | Why durations change: the transited star is itself moving across the sky; when it moves the same way as the planet the transit is long, when it moves opposite the transit is short (TOI-1338: "different durations"). Depth changes when the planet crosses the other star or crosses at a different chord (TIC 172900988: transits of primary and secondary 5 d apart). | Kostov et al. 2020, 2021; NASA GSFC release ("vary in depth and duration due to the stars' orbital motion") | VERIFIED (that they vary) / explanation = standard geometry | 2026-10-19 |
| C9 | Circumbinary transit probability (over time) is always higher than for a single star at the same orbit and depends only weakly on planet period; e.g. a planet at 1 AU around a 0.3 AU eclipsing binary is certain to eventually transit if mutual inclination > 0.6°. | Martin & Triaud 2015, arXiv:1501.03631; Martin 2018 (full) | LIKELY (abstract) / VERIFIED (Martin 2018 statement) | 2026-10-19 |
| C10 | Orbital (nodal) precession makes transits switch on and off: Kepler-413 b precesses in ~11 yr, "stretches of hundreds of days with no transits"; Kepler-453 b precesses in ~103 yr and transits only ~8% of the time ("for every system like [this] that we detect, there are ~12 ... not currently exhibiting transits"); Kepler-1661 b precesses in 35 yr, transiting ~7% of the time. | arXiv:1401.7275; arXiv:1409.1605; arXiv:2001.02840 | LIKELY (abstracts) | 2026-10-19 |
| C11 | TOI-1338 b: nodal precession period 21.4 yr; transits possible in two ~1000-day windows per cycle; planet transits 29.7% of the time; misses begin ~2021-04-26, ~30% missed from 2023-05-20, and after 2025-09-12 transits have ≲30% chance. NASA's release simplified this to "cease after November 2023 and resume eight years later". | Kostov et al. 2020 (full); NASA GSFC release | VERIFIED (both; paper preferred) | 2026-10-19 |
| C12 | Very tight binaries lack transiting CBPs: none around binaries with P < 7 d although the Kepler EB median is 2.8 d; "at least twice as many should have been discovered" if planets ignored binary period. | Martin 2018 (full); Martin, Mazeh & Fabrycky 2015, arXiv:1505.05749; Armstrong et al. 2014 | VERIFIED / LIKELY | 2026-10-19 |
| C13 | Proposed explanations for the <7 d desert: binary shrinkage via a third star (Kozai-Lidov cycles + tidal friction) that destabilises or misaligns planets (Martin et al. 2015; Muñoz & Lai 2015); tidal shrinkage lowering transit probability (Mogan & Zanazzi 2024); resonance-driven instability in multi-planet systems (Liu & Lai 2026). Planets could still exist there on wide or misaligned orbits, better found by eclipse timing (Li et al. 2016). | arXiv:1505.05749, 1505.05514, 2412.01616, 2603.00547, 1608.01768 | LIKELY (abstracts) | 2026-10-19 |
| C14 | Light-travel-time eclipse timing: the binary circles the system's centre of mass, so eclipses arrive early/late by (reflex radius)/c. My numbers: TOI-1338 c ≈ 0.055 s; 1 M_J at 1 yr around 1.5 M_sun ≈ 0.36 s; 5 M_J at 10 yr ≈ 8.4 s (edge-on). | derived (definition + Kepler III), script in report | VERIFIED (derived) | 2026-10-19 |
| C15 | With TESS alone, ETVs mostly find brown dwarfs, not planets: 5% recovery for circumbinary brown dwarfs vs 0.1% for Jupiter-like planets; smallest detectable 1.6 M_J at 1860 d. | Esmer & Daylan 2025, arXiv:2503.20900 | LIKELY (abstract) | 2026-10-19 |
| C16 | Apsidal precession of the binary can reveal non-transiting CBPs (Baycroft et al. 2023 method; Thornton et al. 2025 applied to TESS); tidal/rotational/GR precession must be subtracted and stellar tides are a systematic (Jiang 2026). | arXiv:2301.11372 (title), 2512.07934, 2608.13269 (title) | LIKELY | 2026-10-19 |
| C17 | TESS sectors are ~month-long (Sector 21 ran 2020-01-21 to 2020-02-18). Since every known transiting CBP has P ≥ 49 d, a single sector usually catches at most one conjunction; but at one conjunction a planet can transit 2+ times within days (both stars, or the same star twice), which gives a period estimate within <10 to 20% for low-eccentricity orbits. | C19 | Occurrence: Armstrong et al. 2014: 10 (+18/−6.5)% for R > 6 R_E, P < 300 d if coplanar (≥47% if isotropic); Martin & Triaud 2014: minimum ~9%, comparable to single stars; Welsh et al. 2012: >~1% of close binaries host coplanar giants. | arXiv:1404.5617, 1404.5360, 1204.3955; Martin 2018 (full) | LIKELY / VERIFIED | 2026-10-19 |
