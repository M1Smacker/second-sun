# Other teams' methods and STANLEY feasibility

## Evidence, checked 13 Nov 2026

CONFIRMED from primary sources, not a performance claim for Second Sun. Short exact extracts are used below; the method descriptions are paraphrases.

STANLEY: the paper describes an "iterative cosine filter" and says "For every set of circumbinary parameters the N-body code outputs a sequence of transit times and durations". The second detrending pass uses variable biweight windows. Predicted transits are locally adjusted and stacked with varying intervals and durations. This is a physical orbit search, not our single-event box detector. The authors report hundreds of computational hours for broad searches with over 100 million orbital simulations. Those are their workloads, not a benchmark on this PC. [Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03186), [method, sections II.6, II.7, III.3 and III.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2101.03186v1).

mono-cbp: exact extracts are "a total of 21 light curves" and "3 consecutive cadences lie below a threshold determined by the MAD". Cosine detrending is followed by 21 biweight windows from 1 to 3 days, spaced by 0.1 day. Its search uses 30-minute samples and a local scatter threshold. Its published vetting normally requires detection in at least 18 windows. Consequently, simply accepting any of three windows is an exploratory sensitivity test, not a reproduction of mono-cbp or its false-alarm protection. [Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09435), [method, sections 2.2 and 2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09435v1).

CONFIRMED: both codebases are public. STANLEY's current repository is [TKleanthousT/stanley_cbp](https://github.com/TKleanthousT/stanley_cbp), with an [MIT licence](https://github.com/TKleanthousT/stanley_cbp/blob/main/License.txt). Its [maintainer's package page](https://pypi.org/project/stanley-cbp/) independently states MIT. mono-cbp is [bdrdavies/mono-cbp](https://github.com/bdrdavies/mono-cbp), with a [GNU GPL version 3 licence](https://github.com/bdrdavies/mono-cbp/blob/main/LICENSE). Neither package was installed. No external implementation was copied into the harness.

## Fixed minimal multi-window study

Implementation clarification before any alternate-window result: run the full existing detection and vetting separately with 1-day and 3-day biweight defaults, retaining the already measured 2-day route. Accept a planted transit if any route qualifies. This unions separately vetted routes, not unvetted candidates before their cross-event flags. All eclipse masks, safe windows, box durations, matching radius, tier rules, original draws and paired bump controls remain fixed. Zero-injection identity is checked with the original 2-day route before changing the detrending default. Modified output belongs only to data/astra/003/study-w1 and study-w3.

Selection: original dip failures that survived the cadence/mask diagnostics but produced no matched event, stage 3 in the journal. These are detection-stage failures, not proof that detrending alone caused the loss. Trials without usable windows or whose centre lands in a gap are explicitly reported as unrepaired: changing a detrending scale cannot create missing data. Mask-stage failures and already detected tier failures are outside this requested minimal comparison. Both signs are rerun for every selected dip failure, even when its original bump succeeded. This selected-failure sample cannot furnish a new unbiased overall dip/bump test or an independent validation. Source: 003_study.py and the optional study-window route in 003_worker.py.

## STANLEY feasibility, no build

CONFIRMED by 003_feasibility.py, using only allowed target metadata and stored group filenames: all 704 unique design stars have groups, and 703 have at least two sectors. G2 has 420 stars, 419 multi-sector, 348 with at least five sectors, and 7253 groups. G3 has 284 stars, all multi-sector, 193 with at least five sectors, and 2476 groups. These are availability counts, not 703 scientifically ready stacks. No light curves were opened by this census.

A restricted stack would need consistent sector-normalized time and flux, masks, uncertainties, binary period and epoch, component masses and radii, binary eccentricity and orientation with uncertainties, and a predeclared planet-period/phase/eccentricity grid. Multi-sector availability does not guarantee two planet transits fall in observed windows. Widely separated sectors make phase propagation and parameter errors important. Catalogue periods alone cannot supply a reliable orbital solution.

NOT TESTED: how many of the 703 have sufficiently measured binary parameters, a useful transit baseline, or recoverable multiple transits. No STANLEY search was run and no local runtime was measured. A restricted runtime estimate must be built as stars times orbital templates times measured integration-and-score cost, divided by the allowed workers, plus detrending. The template cost must be benchmarked on synthetic or published systems before promising hours. The paper's full-search compute expense makes a blanket 703-star run unjustified. The next implementation decision would be a small, preregistered feasibility pilot after the current chain's recovery problem is understood; this brief does not authorize building it.
