# RS-037 prep: the multi-crossing (combined-transit) search, can it carry P to 0.75? (Claude, 15 Nov 2026, 6:40 pm Kuwait; WAITS for Ahmed's approval)

## The decision it serves
Whether to build a search that adds up the weak dips of one planet across all its crossings, instead of needing one crossing strong enough alone (SNR 7). It decides Fable's new task F4 on Monday and the road to the new bar (`research/stage3/G1-PLANET-BAR-75-2026-11-15.md`: P at least 0.75, lower bound at least 0.65).

## Why it was not done earlier (plain)
The 13 Nov bar already allowed a combined method, but it was never built: we tried the cheaper single-crossing repairs first (masks, windows, dump rules), and a circumbinary planet's crossings do not come at a fixed rhythm (the stars move, so each crossing shifts in time and length), which makes adding them up much harder than for an ordinary planet.

## Questions, in order, each tied to a choice
1. **Ceiling, from our own bench first (decides whether 0.75 is even in reach).** Of the 1,098 reference planets, how many have 2 or more crossings in the observed data, and how many missed planets would pass SNR 7 if their crossings were added (SNR grows about as the square root of the count for equal dips)? Script on Astra's 006 bench output; if it does not store per-crossing SNR, estimate from depth and each sector's measured scatter, labelled as estimate. Output: an upper limit for P from combining.
2. **Methods in the literature (decides which design Fable judges).** Which published searches combine crossings for circumbinary planets, how they predict where crossings fall, and how they control false alarms. Leads to check on primary pages (not yet verified): CB-BLS (Ofir 2008), the automated Kepler search of Windemuth and co-workers (2019), QATS (Carter and Agol 2013), recent TESS circumbinary searches (Kostov and co-workers). Pull the exact sentences; label each.
3. **False alarms (decides whether it can pass Tree rule 1).** Searching many possible orbits means many tries; how big does the upside-down alarm count get, and how do the papers calibrate it? Our upside-down arm is the test.
4. **Cost on our machine (decides whether it fits before G1).** Orbits to try per star, run time on the bench, what can reuse our existing per-sector detrended light.
5. **Second road, cheap check:** would a looser single-crossing tier (for example SNR 6) plus a "same planet seen twice" rule give part of the gain more simply? Read from the same ceiling script.
