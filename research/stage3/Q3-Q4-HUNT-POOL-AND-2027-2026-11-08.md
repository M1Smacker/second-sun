- **Why slow detached binaries are rare** (arithmetic, equal Sun-like pair): eclipse chance about (R1 + R2) / a is 18% at 3 d, 13% at 5 d, 8% at 10 d, 5% at 20 d, 2.8% at 50 d, 1.8% at 100 d. Our own S1 to 26 2-minute shares (`eb-share-by-coverage.txt`): EBs are 1.2% to 4% of stars, G2-type (bright, detached, P over 5 d) 0.3% to 1%. New post-sector-26 pool: 258,858 stars brighter than Tmag 13.5, expected 5,842 EBs and about 1,376 G2-type (`CENSUS-STEP1-COUNTS-2026-11-04.md`).
- **Pool (Gaia DR3, P over 5 d):** 188,407 in total, mostly faint (CONFIRMED count); sampled estimate 59,000 brighter than G 17, 20,800 brighter than G 15, 11,600 brighter than G 14, 5,500 brighter than G 13 (UNCERTAIN, factor 1.5 to 2). See `G5-SIZING-2026-11-08.md`. A 100k list exists on paper only by adding P 3 to 5 d and stars down to G 17.
- **Not limited to TESS for finding circumbinary planets, but limited to TESS and Kepler for transits** (CONFIRMED from our NASA Exoplanet Archive download, 53 rows with cb_flag = 1, `data\wave-1\nea_pscomppars_cbflag1.csv`): 12 discovered in transit by Kepler, 2 by TESS; eclipse timing about 17, direct imaging 12, microlensing 6, radial velocity 3, pulsar timing 1. Hosts must be eclipsing for coplanar transiting planets (eclipse range is wider for the tighter pair), so eclipsing binaries are the right pool (LIKELY, from geometry).
