# G2 star list: counts of each choice (29 Oct 2026)

Made from `data/xmatch/ebs.csv`, the TESS eclipsing binary table (Prsa et al. 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.13382) that we already had on this machine from 27 Oct. So no catalogue download was needed. Nothing was searched, no light curve was downloaded and no event was opened. Counts only.

## What the table holds
- 4,584 rows, 4,578 different TIC stars (6 stars appear twice). CONFIRMED by counting the file. The 4,584 is also the number in the paper's abstract (CONFIRMED, opened 28 Oct).
- The `Sectors` column of this saved copy reaches sector 39, and 844 stars list a sector above 26 (CONFIRMED by counting). The paper covers sectors 1 to 26, so the saved copy has later sectors added to the same 4,584 stars (its `UpDate` column is later than its `Date` column). Whether the periods and morphology numbers were redone with the later data is UNCERTAIN. The sector counts in the table below already include those later sectors.
- Only **53 of our 549 G1 stars** are in this table. G1 sits in the Kepler field, which TESS saw in only a few of sectors 1 to 26. So G2 built from this table is mostly NEW sky, not leftovers of G1.
- 261 stars in the table are TOI hosts. 11 are NASA circumbinary planet hosts (CONFIRMED by matching `data/xmatch/cbp.csv`). Seven of those 11 are detached with a period over 5 days, but only 3 are Tmag 13.5 or brighter, and one of the 3 (Kepler-1647) is already in G1. So about 2 known hosts would sit in G2 as labelled checks (one is very likely TOI-1338, binary period 14.6 d; LIKELY, matched by period only).

- The professionals (Davies and colleagues) reach 591 stars at period over 7 d, because they first dropped 588 ambiguous stars and later removed 6 circumstellar hosts by eye. My 748 is a plain count without those steps, so it is larger. CONFIRMED for their numbers (our note `research/audit-2026-10-28-varwise-mono-cbp.md`); the exact overlap with my 748 is NOT TESTED.
- Of the 748 detached stars over 7 d, 288 have only one sector (so one 27 day look).

## My recommendation (a proposal, not decided)
**Use the G1 rule on the new table:** detached, binary period over 5 days, Tmag 13.5 or brighter, not in G1. That is **895 stars**. Reasons: the frozen v7 settings were never tuned on these stars; the rule is the same one that defined G1, so G2 tests the same kind of population and not a cut chosen after looking; and it is larger than the professionals' list, so it includes stars they did not search.
- Keep the 1 sector stars in; report results by number of sectors.
- Mark the 11 known circumbinary hosts (and any TOI) as labelled checks, never deleted.
- Split by a fixed rule written down before any search: the last digit of a hash of the TIC number picks the slice (for example about one third development, two thirds sealed). The sealed slice is searched once, after the rules and the hash are committed.

My earlier guess of 1 to 4 GB was WRONG: I had used the table's own sector column (median 2), but MAST holds every sector TESS has released since, not only those in the table.
- All 895 stars have light curves in all three products (SPOC, TESS-SPOC and QLP). None is QLP only, unlike G1 where 237 of 549 stars were.
- Files to fetch: SPOC 9,505, TESS-SPOC 8,155, QLP 10,127, **27,787 in total** (G1 had 8,100). Sectors per star: median 7, 90th percentile 40, only 9 stars with a single sector. CONFIRMED by the query.
- **Estimated download about 28 GB** (G1 file sizes times G2 file counts; G1 itself was 5.8 GB for 8,100 files). About 9 to 10 GB per third. UNCERTAIN: the SPOC count may include 20 second files next to 2 minute ones, which would make it smaller; the file sizes are borrowed from G1. The disk has 490 GB free, so space is no problem.

## Update after Ahmed's question "have these stars already been searched?" (29 Oct 2026, same day)
- CONFIRMED by matching TIC numbers (counts only): **531 of the 895 G2 stars (59%) are in the professionals' 591-star table** (`TEBC_morph_05_P_7.csv`, held outside this repository). 178 of the 301 development stars and 353 of the 594 sealed stars. All 531 have a period over 7 days; none of the 228 stars with a period of 5 to 7 days can be in their table. 364 stars are not in it.
- They searched 512 of their 591 after removals we cannot see, with a different tool (mono-cbp, 30 minute TESS-SPOC flux). So for most of G2 our run is a second look with a different method, a useful cross-check but not a first look.
- Other labels: 95 TOIs (29 development, 66 sealed), 1 NASA-listed circumbinary host (sealed). 310 stars are in none of the three groups (107 development, 203 sealed): the least covered ones.
