# Codex 014 part 3: second key on the deep-planet bench and the depth cap (FIX 96)

Claude, 15 Nov 2026, after Codex's final commit 7d2e6a3c (1:31 am). Node 1.6. Codex report: `D:\second-sun-astra\research\astra\reports\014-dumps-ephemerides-deep-bench.md` (branch astra/014-dumps-ephemerides, not merged).

**Plain verdict:** the depth cap (FIX 96) costs no planted planet and removes about a third of the background alarms in both arms. It is a safe physics guard like FIX 95, not a gain in planets. Not adopted; goes to the Fable round with the rest of 014.

## What was tested
Codex planted 600 deep planets (120 per binary-period bin, seed 2026111416, depth drawn up to the largest depth a 2 Jupiter-radius planet can make on that host's TIC radius) into the raw light, ran the adopted filter B, and asked whether a cap on the *measured* event depth (hold any event deeper than 1,000,000 × min(1, (2 R_Jup / R_star)²) ppm) loses any of them, and what it does to the background.

## What it means
- **Background after the cap:** 124 real / 53 upside-down (was 174 / 79). Upside-down alarms fall by 26 (33%) at no planet cost. The real excess (real minus upside-down) falls from 95 to 71: 24 real events go that no planet could make, because they are deeper than a 2 Jupiter-radius body can block on that star. LIKELY unmasked eclipse leftovers.
- **Binned (pooled ratios can hide splits):** held real / upside-down by period bin 10/33 and 12/25, 9/26 and 6/17, 21/46 and 5/11, 4/10 and 2/8, 6/43 and 1/16; by product QLP 35/56 and 14/32, SPOC 2/40 and 6/23, TESS-SPOC 12/47 and 5/16. No bin where the cap removes real events but spares upside-down ones in a way the counts can tell apart. Nearly all its work is on events of 15,000 ppm and deeper (46 of 52 real, 18 of 25 upside-down).
- **Tree rule:** same planets caught (264 dips), fewer upside-down alarms (79 to 53). Like FIX 95 it passes as a safety fix, not as a planet gain.

## What this bench cannot show (UNCERTAIN / NOT TESTED)
- **It is partly circular.** The same TIC radius sets the planted depth and the cap, so 0 lost dips only shows the detector does not inflate depth. The real risk is a host whose TIC radius is too large (a blended binary, or a star wrongly fitted as evolved), which makes the cap too low. The 2 Jupiter-radius margin (4× the area of Jupiter) covers an equal-twin blend; a radius 2 to 3× too large would not be covered. Next check: count how many design-seen hosts have a TIC radius above 2 to 3 solar radii and whether their own eclipse depths agree.
- **Checked right after (same night):** half of what the cap holds sits on hosts the catalogue calls 2 solar radii or larger, exactly where a too-large radius would bite. Held / events by TIC radius, real: under 1: 5/45, 1 to 2: 20/65, 2 to 3: 19/38, 3 and up: 6/10; upside-down: 1/19, 7/26, 15/23, 3/9. 14 held events on hosts of 2 or more solar radii are shallower than 30,000 ppm; the shallowest held event is 1,857 ppm (only possible on a host above 4.6 solar radii). So before adoption the radii of these hosts need an independent check (Gaia DR3 radius, the binary's own eclipse widths). Script: inline check, numbers from `deep-g2.json`, `deep-g3.json` and `targets/host_radii_g2_g3.csv`.
- Dilution, limb darkening and radius error are not modelled (Codex says so too).
- The 16 real events with no usable radius stay unguarded.
