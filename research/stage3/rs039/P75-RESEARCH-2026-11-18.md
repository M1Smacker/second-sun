### A2. Is the noise in the light or in what detrending leaves? (CONFIRMED as computed, `a2_noise_floor.py`, `a2b_photon.py`)
- Our per-hour white noise sits a median 1.27 (SPOC) to 1.35 (QLP) times above the cleanest 10% of our own stars at the same brightness and product, and 1.6 to 1.9 times the star's own photon noise at Tmag 9 to 11 (2.0 to 3.0 at Tmag 12 to 13; photon rate 15,000 e-/s at Tmag 10, HEASARC TESS instrument page, CONFIRMED; duty 0.8 assumed). Sky, read noise and pointing jitter (about 60 ppm per hour, same page) live in that factor and cannot all be removed.
- The search's noise for a box is a median **2.1 to 2.5 times** what the white noise predicts (the red factor), and **3.0 times for weak crossings** (IQR 2.0 to 4.2). Depth kept by the frozen detrend: median 0.74 for weak crossings.
- Weak crossings that would reach SNR 7: light at the best-star level 45%; white noise 67%; both 85%.
- **Reading:** the biggest removable loss is the red part (hours-long wiggles left by a 2-day window, or carried by the light), then lost depth. Better photometry is a smaller lever (factor about 1.3 on the median star, more for faint QLP stars).
- Pipeline fact (CONFIRMED, `pipeline/detect_v2.py` lines 173 and 201 to 218): detrend = wotan biweight with a fixed 2.0-day window for every box length 4 to 20 h; SNR = depth over 1.4826 x MAD of ALL box means of the star-sector (one noise number per star-sector and box length).

## Part B: primary sources (exact sentences in NOTES-RAW.md)

### B1. Detrending that keeps depth and removes wiggles
- **wotan (Hippke et al. 2019, CONFIRMED):** biweight wins; best window **3 x T14**; "Simply choosing a unique long window length for all searches would result in sub-optimal detrending ... improved transit search algorithms should include a custom detrending for each period." Our 2-day window is 2.4 to 12 times the box length.
- **Martin and Fabrycky 2021, STANLEY (CONFIRMED):** circumbinary durations vary up to ten-fold; window chosen PER STAR by a whiteness test: shorten the window until the residual periodogram has no power above 1% false-alarm at periods longer than the longest transit.
- **Oddo et al. 2026b, TESS M-dwarf binaries (CONFIRMED, submitted 15 Oct 2026):** 48 copies with windows from 3 x shortest to 0.75 x 3 x longest predicted duration; each point taken from the copy matching the duration predicted at that time; kink removal; per-sector noise.
- **Notch filter (Rizzuto et al. 2017, CONFIRMED):** quadratic plus box notch fitted together in a 0.5 to 1 day window, BIC decides whether the notch is used: the dip never enters its own baseline.
- **PLATO comparison (2023, CONFIRMED):** Notch-and-LOCoR "misses the lowest number of transits"; the union of two detrenders recovers most.
- **Kepler TPS (Twicken et al. 2016, CONFIRMED):** harmonics fitted and removed before the search ("significantly reduces the number of false alarms"); noise estimated per time with a moving MAD in each wavelet band (time-local noise); quarter-by-quarter whitening because false alarms crowded segment edges.
- **What this means for us:** our c1a test (3 x box window) was the right idea in the field's terms; it failed at OUR tier level because shorter windows carve side bumps that our mirror flag reads as an upside-down match (X11: 105 of 133 lost dips). The fix the literature points to is a statistic that never puts the dip in its own baseline (notch), or a per-star window chosen by whiteness (STANLEY), not a longer window.

### B2. Eclipses: mask or subtract?
mono-cbp masks by phase because subtraction "can create transit-like features" (RS-038, CONFIRMED); CB-BLS on CoRoT subtracted a model (search summary, LIKELY). The mask pool is 5.3 points (A3). Not a top lever; FIX 101 and M2 already own it.

### B3. Signal-to-noise with red noise
Our SNR already uses the empirical scatter of box means, so it is honest about red noise; it is GLOBAL per star-sector. TPS (moving MAD per time and scale), Oddo 2026b (per sector) and LEO-Vetter (white plus red term after Hartman and Bakos) all measure noise more locally or by component. Gain from time-local noise: measured in A6 (below).

### B4. Photometry
QLP replaced its photometry with TGLC (Gaia-based, deblended) from Sector 94: "better noise characteristics ... from 10.5 < T < 13.5" (QLP DRN 004, CONFIRMED; size of the gain only in a figure). TGLC light curves exist for earlier sectors as a separate product (Han and Brandt 2023, search summary). Our A2 says this lever is about a factor 1.3 on white noise for the median star: real but second order.

### B5. A second false-alarm meter
- **Kepler DR25 (Thompson et al. 2018, CONFIRMED):** reliability measured with TWO simulated false-alarm sets, inverted AND scrambled light, because "In order for inversion to exactly reproduce the false alarm population, the false alarms would need to be perfectly symmetric ... which is not true". Skye: events binned in 1-day bins per skygroup; an excess of events at one time marks artefacts.
- **Planet Hunters TESS (Eisner et al. 2020, CONFIRMED):** a histogram of volunteer marks across all light curves of a sector; many marks at one time = systematics.
- **mono-cbp (RS-038):** a TESS Skye costing "a few per cent" of planted transits.
- **What this means for us:** the field's answer to "the upside-down twin cannot see dip-only artefacts" is a second meter. Our upside-down twin stays; add a **witness-star meter**: many unrelated stars on the same camera and CCD in the same sector, searched the same way; dips that pile up at one time are artefacts, and their rate is measured directly. Our own `pipeline/commonmode_check.py` already does a symmetric cross-star count, but only across OUR few stars per CCD; a pool of a few hundred random non-target stars per CCD and sector would make it strong (download size NOT yet priced). This meter is what could let DS, M2 and R2 (+2 to +4 points, blocked by rule 4) back in honestly.

### B6. One score instead of many holds
- **DR25 disposition score (CONFIRMED):** the Robovetter wrapped in a Monte Carlo over perturbed metrics; the threshold is chosen on the completeness/reliability curve.
- **Foreman-Mackey et al. 2016 (CONFIRMED):** single events judged by model comparison (BIC) of a transit model against box, step and variability models; conservative, costs low-SNR events.
- **LEO-Vetter (Kunimoto et al. 2025, CONFIRMED):** individual-event tests (Chases, data-coverage, per-event chi-square) are portable; its multi-transit logic is not.
- **ML (NotPlaNET, CONFIRMED):** "human vetting still significantly outperforms current models" for single transits; a CNN removed a median 18% of contaminants at under 1% planet loss.
- **What this means for us:** the held pool (16.7 points) is a tier-design problem. A single calibrated score (event features: shape fit against step and ramp models, flank behaviour, dump distance, coverage, witness coincidence, mirror match), trained on planted planets against BOTH meters and judged on a fresh bench at a fixed alarm budget, is the field's way. Not tested here.

### B7. What the best searches reach (CONFIRMED)
Per transit: mono-cbp 0.41 to 0.61; Oddo 2026b at most about 0.60 ("bounded around ~60% because of ... inherent noise ..., signal attenuation from our multi-step de-trending ..., the removal of sectors where more than 3 unique signals passed ..., and the requirement that the recovered depths and durations match injected values to within 40%"). Oddo 2026b uses SNR 10, not 7, because 7.3 "passed too many signals that were not real". Our per-crossing 0.36 (A7) is below both.

### B8. Stacking crossings
A1: stacking adds 6.5 points of detection at SNR 7 but only 1.6 once a fair penalty for trying many orbits is paid (8.5). Codex 028 measured under 1 point with a strict null floor. Low priority until noise falls.
