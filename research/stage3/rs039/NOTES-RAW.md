## Sources
- Twicken et al. 2016, DR25 TPS (ar5iv 1604.06140), CONFIRMED text: TPS is an adaptive wavelet matched filter; "harmonic signals are fitted and removed from each light curve on a quarter-by-quarter basis prior to conducting the transit search. This process significantly reduces the number of false alarms"; noise per wavelet bandpass from a "nondecimated (in time) moving median absolute deviation (MAD) filter"; quarter-by-quarter whitening because false alarms crowded segment boundaries; threshold MES 7.1 plus chi-square and robust-statistic vetoes.
- Hippke et al. 2019, wotan (ar5iv 1906.00966), CONFIRMED text: biweight time-windowed slider is the winner (6 misses of 100 lowest-SNR Kepler planets); "the highest minimum (and mean) SDE is achieved for a window of size 3 T14. This choice recovers all planets"; white noise allows w/T14 >= 2.2 to keep >= 98% of the flux integral; "Simply choosing a unique long window length for all searches would result in sub-optimal detrending, leading to reduced sensitivity ... improved transit search algorithms should include a custom detrending for each period." Our window is a fixed 2.0 d for boxes of 4 to 20 h: 2 to 12 T14, so too long for short boxes (leaves wiggles) and the c1a lesson says short windows raise the mirror flag (side bumps): the mirror check must change with the window. Also: simultaneous trend + transit modelling (Foreman-Mackey 2015 K2) named as an alternative, benefit "still unclear" in 2019.
- Rizzuto et al. 2017, ZEIT V (ar5iv 1709.09670), CONFIRMED text: notch filter = "a quadratic polynomial in time with a box-shaped transit notch [fit] to a short (0.5-1 day) window of data centered on the transit notch"; BIC between quadratic-only and quadratic+notch, notch applied only when evidence supports it; "the method is robust to falsely identifying in-transit points" because in-transit points leave the variability fit; limit: the transit must not span a large fraction of the window, else degeneracy; recovered all 10 known young-cluster planets where no earlier single pipeline did. Relevance: lets the window be short (removing hour-scale wiggles) WITHOUT carving side bumps or eating the dip, i.e. exactly c1a's two failures.
- PLATO activity-filter comparison (arXiv 2302.02183 html), CONFIRMED text: comparing Notch and LOCoR, YSD, K2SC, VARLET, biweight, Huber spline: "N&L is the best choice in many cases, since it misses the lowest number of transits", underperforms when planet period near rotation period; "the combined results of two algorithms, YSD and Huber spline, yield the highest recovery percentage". So: union of two detrenders recovers most (the opposite of mono-cbp's 18-of-21 agreement rule, which is their biggest planet killer).
- HEASARC TESS instrument performance page (heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/tess/telescope_information.html), CONFIRMED text: "The TESS cameras create 15,000 e-/s for a star of Tmag = 10"; "typical photometric uncertainties are dominated by pointing jitter, which are at the level of 60 ppm on hourly timescales"; requirement 230 ppm at Tmag 10 per hour.
- A2b own data (a2b_photon.txt, CONFIRMED as computed; duty 0.8 assumed): per-hour noise over the star's own photon noise, median: SPOC 1.6 to 1.7 at T9 to T11, 2.0 to 2.1 at T12 to T13; QLP 1.8 to 1.9 at T9 to T11, 2.5 to 3.0 at T12 to T13. Weak crossings 2.6 (IQR 2.1 to 3.6). - QLP DRN 004 (arXiv 2603.22236, search summary only, NOT yet opened): QLP switched to TGLC photometry from Sector 94, "better noise characteristics" at 10.5 < T < 13.5. TGLC paper Han and Brandt 2023 (arXiv 2301.03704, search summary): PSF-based, Gaia-deblended, 2% at T16 in 30 min.
- QLP DRN 004 (arXiv 2603.22236 html), CONFIRMED text: QLP replaced its photometry with TGLC (Gaia priors, ePSF forward model removing neighbour contamination), "better noise characteristics than the original QLP photometry from 10.5 < T < 13.5", main photometry always a 3x3 TGLC aperture; summary: "improved precision across the full range of covered brightness". Size of the gain is in a figure only (ratio plot), not read as a number (UNCERTAIN size).
- Thompson et al. 2018, DR25 KOI catalog (ar5iv 1710.06758), CONFIRMED text: reliability measured with TWO false-alarm sets, inverted light curves and scrambled light curves (years reordered, seasons kept so yearly artefacts survive); they say plainly inversion is imperfect: "In order for inversion to exactly reproduce the false alarm population, the false alarms would need to be perfectly symmetric ... which is not true"; individual-transit tests Rubble, Marshall, Chases, Skye, Zuma, Tracker; Skye: "for each skygroup we bin the individual events into 1.0 d bins", an excess of events at the same time on the same detectors marks artefacts; disposition score = the Robovetter wrapped in a Monte Carlo over perturbed metrics, a 0 to 1 confidence used to trade completeness against reliability. Relevance: (1) the field's answer to "the inverted twin is blind to some artefacts" is a SECOND, different meter; (2) the field ranks by one score and picks the threshold from the completeness/reliability curve, instead of hard holds.
- Oddo, Dragomir, Martin, Tubthong, Han 2026, CBP occurrence around TESS M-dwarf EBs (arXiv 2610.22625, submitted 15 Oct 2026; abstract and html read), CONFIRMED text: first TESS CBP occurrence rate (95% upper limit 33.0% coplanar, 2 to 16 Earth radii, P up to 600 d); variable-duration detrend after Martin and Fabrycky section 2.7: "48 copies, with each copy being de-trended with a different window length" from 3x min to 0.75 x 3x max predicted duration, each point taken from the copy whose window matches the transit duration predicted at that time; a kink-removal stage (hooks at gaps, jumps); per-sector noise ("more sensitive to smaller signals in sectors with lower intrinsic noise levels"); data within 0.5 d of gaps masked; SNR threshold 10 (7.3 "passed too many signals that were not real"); LEO-vetter with multi-transit tests disabled; injection > 1 million transits; "Our detection sensitivity is bounded around ~60% because of ... inherent noise ..., signal attenuation from our multi-step de-trending ..., the removal of sectors where more than 3 unique signals passed the detection threshold, and the requirement that the recovered depths and durations match injected values to within 40%." 302 events from 7,422 sector light curves, 207 false alarms, 1 new candidate.
- So the published TESS single-event state of the art is about 0.41 to 0.61 per transit (mono-cbp) and about 0.60 per transit at best (Oddo 2026b). Our P is per planet (several crossings), so it is not directly comparable; a per-crossing number for us is needed (A7 below).
- Martin and Fabrycky 2021, STANLEY (arXiv 2101.03186 html), CONFIRMED text: CBP durations vary "by almost an order of magnitude" at one period, so one window either eats long transits or leaves wiggles; their per-star adaptive rule: detrend (cosine filter) with window 3 x tau_max, compute the residual periodogram, and if any power above a 1% false-alarm level remains at periods longer than tau_max, shorten the window by 0.5 x tau_max and redo from the original light curve, down to 1 x tau_max, then switch to tau_75%; stop rather than filter transits away. Relevance: a per-star-sector window chosen by a whiteness test on the residuals, a principled answer to our red factor (2 to 3, different per star) that does not need planet times.
- Kunimoto et al. 2025, LEO-Vetter (arXiv 2509.10619 html), CONFIRMED text: SNR uses white AND red noise (sigma_r after Hartman and Bakos); SWEET sine test, Chases (does the event stand out from the local SES series), Model-Shift, individual-transit tests (chi-square per transit, Rubble-like data-coverage test); requires N_tr >= 3 and SNR > 6.2 after dropping bad transits, so it is built for periodic signals; Skye not implemented (Kepler-specific). Search summary (not opened): 91% of injected planets kept, 99.91% of simulated false alarms rejected; NEMESIS (arXiv 2611.21758) measured 0.835 completeness and 0.818 reliability at the published thresholds (lower than headline). Relevance: individual-event tests (Chases, Rubble, chi-square per event) are portable to single events; the multi-transit logic is not.
- A7 per-crossing efficiency (a7_per_crossing.txt, a7b_per_crossing_direct.txt; CONFIRMED as computed): direct count on the Astra 006 bench (older chain, P about 0.48): a planted crossing with usable data reaches tier 1 36.2% of the time (per bin 25, 35, 34, 41, 47); 30.0% over all observed crossings. - Foreman-Mackey et al. 2016, long-period single transits in Kepler (ar5iv 1607.08237), CONFIRMED text: single events vetted by MODEL COMPARISON (BIC) of a physical transit model against parameterised systematics models (box, step, variability and others); "The box model is the most restrictive comparison, vetoing about half of the candidate events"; "This step is conservative: unless a signal is a very convincing transit, it won't pass the test ... all but the highest signal-to-noise events will be rejected"; completeness from 819,752 injections. Relevance: model comparison is the field's alternative to yes/no flags for single events (mono-cbp uses AIC for steps); it is honest but costly at low SNR, so it suits the HELD pool (detected, 16.7 points) better than the too-faint pool.
- NotPlaNET (arXiv 2405.18278v1 html), CONFIRMED text: 1D CNN on Planet Hunters TESS labels, single events, no period; "human vetting still significantly outperforms current models" for long-period, single-transit, aperiodic events; it removed a median 18% (10 to 37%) of contaminants while flagging at most 0.6% of planet candidates. Relevance: ML on single events is a contaminant pre-filter at low recall, not a replacement for our tier rules; a learned score would need our own planted planets and both false-alarm meters as training labels (NOT TESTED).

- A6 RESULT (a6_summary.txt, 160 design stars, 3,051 crossing rows, 2,401 untouched star-sectors, 46,856 days, 0 errors; CONFIRMED as computed):
  - base reproduces X4 (crossings at 7+ 0.659 vs X4 0.627).
  - PREWHITENING (8 sinusoids max, 1 to 48 c/d, S/N>4) does NOT help: weak crossings SNR x0.93 median, only 5% reach 7; it loses 9% of strong crossings; alarms fall on both arms (2.50/1.67 -> 1.61/1.01 per 100 d). The cap of 8 was hit in 55% of star-sectors: it fits noise and part of the dips. Pulsations are not the main red noise (consistent with about 4% of TESS EBs having pulsators, Kahraman Alicavus 2022 via search summary).
  - LOCAL NOISE as I built it is INVALID: alarms x14 on both arms (36/35 per 100 d). Overlapping box means within +-1 d are strongly correlated, so their MAD underestimates the noise. Method bug, not a finding. A fair time-local noise needs independent samples (non-overlapping boxes, wider span) and must be judged at equal alarms.
  - DEPTH: the frozen detrend keeps a median 0.95 of the dip (weak crossings 0.80) measured as mean in-dip flux; a detrend that skips the dip keeps 0.90 (0.82). So lost depth from detrending is SMALL; X4's 'keep' (0.74) is box-level (box grid and trapezoid ingress included). The 'full depth' part of the A1 ceiling is mostly box mismatch, not detrending.
