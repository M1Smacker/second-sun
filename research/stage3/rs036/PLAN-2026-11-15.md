## Questions, each tied to a decision
1. **What should the course cover, and in what order?** Look at real university introductory astronomy and astrophysics courses and their open textbooks (syllabi and tables of contents, primary pages), plus the physics a learner needs on the way (light, gravity, motion, energy, spectra). Decision: the unit and lesson outline (target 36 to 40 lessons in about 9 units), from first principles to circumbinary planets, with side units that are pure wonder (stars' lives, galaxies, cosmology).
2. **Which sources can lessons be built on legally and safely?** For example OpenStax Astronomy 2e (check its licence on the publisher page), NASA and ESA education pages, open course material. Decision: the source policy (what we may adapt, how we credit it).
3. **How do adults who disliked school physics learn it best?** Reuse RS-035 (worked then faded examples, typed recall, data first, concreteness fading) and add evidence on interactive engagement and conceptual inventories in astronomy (for example the Astronomy Diagnostic Test, Lecture-Tutorials). Decision: lesson template changes and how each unit checks understanding.
4. **When is Ahmed ready for the big exam?** Read research/stage3/KEYSTONE-EXAM-SPEC-2026-11-03.md. Decision: which units are required before the exam, and the readiness rule.
5. **Which real data can each unit use?** TESS, Kepler, Gaia, NASA Exoplanet Archive, solar data; never fence A or B stars, never a hunt-list star (the lesson-plot guard). Decision: one hands-on piece per unit.
