# RS-010 Q6: the false-alarm line S2 missed its mark twice. Evidence for Ahmed's decision (night of 4 Nov 2026; research stint RS-011; report only: no decision, nothing changed)

## Plain verdict
**S2 is a borderline reading, not a clear failure: pooled over all three runs the false-alarm rate is 8.6 per 1,000 star-sectors (95% interval 7.1 to 10.3) against a mark of 8, and a true rate of 8 is not ruled out (one-sided chance 0.24).** The two runs flagged FAIL (8.91 and 9.34) each have a chance of 0.21 to 0.22 of arising if the true rate were exactly 8. What looks like a trend (7.1 on bright development stars, 8.9, then 9.3 on faint G3 stars) is within noise, but it points the same way as brightness. The decision is yours: three honest options below, with what each costs.

## What a higher bar does (CONFIRMED counts, LIKELY scaling)
Upside-down tier-1 counts at bars 7, 8 and 10 (S1 tables, PDCSAP half): development 20, 18, 12; sealed 59, 48, 35; G3 31, 21, 13; pooled 110, 87, 60. Scaling the pooled S2 by those ratios gives about **8.6, 6.8 and 4.7 per 1,000** at bars 7, 8 and 10. The completeness price (planted dips with K 8 to 14 kept in tier 1 at that bar): G3 30.5% at bar 7, 21.5% at bar 8, 13.4% at bar 9, 8.4% at bar 10; development 34.7%, 28.0%, 21.4%, 16.6%. So moving from bar 7 to 8 brings S2 under the mark and costs about 7 to 9 points of completeness, 19% to 30% of what we keep.

## Options (for you; my lean marked)
A. **Accept and restate (my lean).** Report S2 as a pooled rate with its interval and no pass/fail, as the borderline-wording rule already written in section D of the benchmark (T40) allows. The mark of 8 was set from the development run before the sealed data existed, so changing the mark now is post hoc; keeping it and reporting 8.6 (7.1 to 10.3) is honest. Costs nothing, changes no frozen file. Weakness: it does not lower false alarms.
B. **Raise the working bar to 8 for Census.** S2 about 6.8, passes; completeness falls about 20% to 30% relative. This is a design change, so it belongs in the Petrova upgrade stage with a new freeze and a new preregistered mark; it also lowers the real-dip yield of the hunt we most care about.
C. **Cut false alarms by linking dips (Census idea 4) or by reviewing a specific check.** Requiring two dips that fit one stable orbit would remove most single chance dips; effect on S2 NOT tested, and idea 4 is not original (see `CENSUS-IDEA4-LITERATURE-2026-11-04.md`), but its use as a false-alarm filter is the point. Reviewing the step check (T34) lowers completeness cost, not necessarily false alarms.
