## 0. What this is, in one line
Horizon measures how big a planet, how far out, the frozen search could have found, per cell, and turns a clean null into a ceiling per cell. **It is an upper bound on what the search could have seen, not a measurement of how common planets are.** Every page that shows it says so.

## 1. Grid (copied from Oddo et al. 2026, arXiv:2610.22625, for comparability)
- 7 planet-radius bins, uniform, 2 to 16 Earth radii (edges 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16). Neptune (3.9) to Jupiter (11.2) is marked on the map; nothing is dropped outside it.
- 7 orbital-period bins, log-uniform, 10 to 600 days (edges 10 x 60^(i/7)).
- Event duration is not a grid axis: it follows from the cell (section 5).
- Star brightness is not an axis; the noise of each star-sector carries it.

## 2. Star sets and the split
- **R1 (recovery, planted dips): the G2 DEVELOPMENT split only (301 stars).** The runner filters `slice == "dev"` and asserts 301 stars and no sealed star. The sealed G2 stars and their result are not touched.
- **Ceilings for G2 sealed and G3:** the recovery table of R1 is applied to the star-sectors of the set (their noise, windows and binary periods), without planting in their light. G3's own completeness table (`chain_recovery.py` after the search, G3 preregistration section 3) is reported beside it; if the two disagree by more than a factor of 2 at the same depth, both are shown and the map uses the G3 one.

## 3. The stability floor (a stated choice, with its sensitivity)
A planet is possible in a cell for a star only if its period is at least F times the star's binary period. **Published map: F = 5.6** (the smallest ratio observed among transiting circumbinary planets, Kepler-16 b at 5.57; the ten shortest observed ratios are 5.57 to 12.87, own table `horizon-research/h2_stability_numbers.txt`). Sensitivity maps: F = 3 (below the theoretical limit of 2.75 to 6.16 of Holman and Wiegert 1999 eq. 3 for e up to 0.3 and mass ratio 0.1 to 0.5) and F = 6.9 (seven of the ten shortest observed ratios lie below it; a pessimistic floor). Stars where the cell is below the floor add nothing to that cell's sum and are not counted as stars that "could have shown" it.

## 4. Geometry
Coplanar, circular planet orbits: the binary is an eclipsing pair, so the plane is edge on and a planet in that plane crosses a star at each pass. A planet at epoch T0 (uniform over one planet period) transits at T0 + k P; the transit is seen if a transit time falls inside the star-sector's good-time windows (section 7). That fraction, from folding the windows modulo the planet period, is the window function W(P) per star-sector. Isotropic planets (Martin 2018, abstract) are NOT in the published map; they get a second map only if time allows, labelled as a scenario.

## 5. Depth and duration of a planted dip
- Depth = (Rp / R_star)^2 with R_star the TIC radius of the star (`targets/host_radii_g2_g3.csv`; 822 of 895 G2 and 562 of 612 G3 positive). **Dilution assumption, stated: the crossed star gives all the light (no dilution).** This makes planets look easier to see than they are: the map is generous, the label says "upper bound". Sensitivity map: the crossed star gives half the light (depth halved). Stars with no valid radius use the median of the set (1.62 solar radii) and are counted separately.
- Duration = P_planet / pi x (R_star + Rp) / a, with a from Kepler's third law for a total mass of 2 solar masses (the same physics as the frozen eclipse-length rule, constants `ECL_MTOT`), capped to Vesper's box range (3 to 20 h). Circumbinary transits vary in timing and duration; the planting spreads duration by a factor drawn log-uniformly from 0.7 to 1.4 (stated, not tuned).
- Shape: trapezoid, 20% ingress and egress, as `chain_recovery.signal` (the frozen planting code, unchanged).

## 6. Recovery rule
Recovered = the planted dip is a **tier 1 event (1A, 1L or 1B) after all flags**, primary product, found by the whole frozen chain (search, second-product check, flags, SAP twin pass, v7 PDCSAP check, union, v7 flags) with `VESPER_OPTS=c6` and nothing else; SNR 7 or more; centre within the match window of the planted time. Exactly as frozen. No new switch, no new threshold. A run that plants nothing must recover nothing (control first).

## 7. Chance and ceiling
- Recovery fraction S(depth x noise, duration) comes from R1 (planted dips at K = 6 to 18 times the 8 h box noise, durations 4, 8, 14, 20 h, as in the frozen completeness runs), interpolated in log K and log duration.
- Detection chance of a star-sector in a cell = W x S. For a star: P(i, c) = 1 minus the product over its star-sectors of (1 minus W x S).
- **Ceiling for zero found: f = -ln(0.05) / sum_i P(i, c) = 3.0 / sum_i P(i, c)** (one-sided 95%, Poisson), the same formula Oddo et al. use. For k found: the standard Poisson upper limit for k events. Ceilings are published per cell; no single occurrence number.
- Window coverage per star-sector: SPOC star-sectors use their own windows; QLP-only star-sectors use the hashed reference windows table (union of G3, G2 and G1 SPOC 2-minute files, `research/stage3/g3-union-windows.json`), as in the search. Star-sectors with no safe windows contribute zero.

## 8. The reality check (before anything is published)
Compare the planted recovery with the real recovery of the known planet TOI-1338 b: 4 of 10 sectors at tier 1 in the sealed run, against the planted completeness at its depth (about 20%, `chain_recovery` sealed table). Pass: planted recovery does not overstate real recovery by more than a factor of 2 at its depth. **Kill rule (written now):** if the planted recovery overstates the real one by more than a factor of 2 for at least 2 known planets and the realism arm (timing and duration spread already in section 5) cannot close the gap, Horizon is stopped and the finding is published as the result. With one known planet in our sets, a pass is weak evidence and is worded that way.

## 9. What is NOT allowed
No tuning on results; if the map looks poor it is published as it is. No edit of the 11 fingerprinted files. No sealed or G3 light is planted into. No event lists, no candidate claims. Labels CONFIRMED, LIKELY, UNCERTAIN, NOT TESTED on every statement; no en or em dashes in book text.

## 10. UNCERTAIN, said now
- Whether circumbinary transit probability for non-coplanar planets (Li et al. 2016, cited in Martin 2018) matters: not read; the published map is coplanar only.
- The depth-to-radius step assumes the TIC radius is the crossed star's and that no light is diluted; both are generous.
- The sealed-run completeness at TOI-1338 b's depth is a 4-of-10 count on one planet.
- Compute time is unknown until one star is timed (R1 begins with that).

Checked on the 284 dev star-sectors: calculated / measured 8 h noise has median 0.98, 16 to 84% range 0.94 to 1.04 (using the best product alone gave 0.93 with 0.64 to 1.00, so it was not used).
3. **Table edges:** S is interpolated in log K and log duration, separately for the SPOC family (SPOC, TESS-SPOC) and QLP as best product; K below 6 gives S = 0 (below the lowest planted depth: understates), K above 18 and durations outside 4 to 20 h use the edge value.
4. **Window function:** W = min(1, covered days / planet period), with covered days = cadences searched x cadence (after windows and eclipse masks). UNCERTAIN: it is an approximation to folding the real windows (exact for a single stretch shorter than the period). A star's chance = 1 minus the product over its star-sectors (independent epochs; UNCERTAIN).
5. **Output:** per star set (G2 development, G2 sealed, G3) and per scenario (main F = 5.6 no dilution; F = 3; F = 6.9; dilution 0.5): `research/stage3/horizon-reach-<set>-<scenario>.txt`, aggregates only. ## Amendment 2 (3 Nov 2026, still before any G3 result; written afterwards for `pipeline/horizon_region.py`, which cites it; no number was tuned)
1. **Region integral.** Region = planets above 6 Earth radii (bins 6 to 16) and 10 to 334 days (period bins 1 to 6), the region of Armstrong et al. 2014 (R > 6 Earth radii, P < 300 d). A star's planet, if it has one, falls uniformly among the cells of the region where that star is allowed (stability floor). D = sum over stars of the mean chance over the star's allowed cells. Ceiling for zero found = 3.0 / D; with the known planet counted (k = 1) = 4.74 / D. This is an assumption about where planets fall, stated, not measured.
2. **Error band:** the recovery table is rebuilt from 30 resamples of the development star-sectors (the run used 30, not the 200 of the file's default; it is the dominant error, the host radii, noise and windows are held).
3. **Result, G2 sealed set (aggregates, `research/stage3/horizon-region-g2sealed.txt`):** main scenario D = 144 stars-equivalent over 585 stars; 95% ceiling 2.1% (band 2.0 to 2.1%) for zero found, 3.3% for k = 1. Other scenarios: F = 3: 1.7% (k = 1: 2.6%); F = 6.9: 2.3% (3.6%); half the light: 2.8% (4.4%).

## Amendment 3 (3 Nov 2026, uncertainty audit RS-006, still before any G3 result): what the window-function test found (`horizon_window_fold_test.py`, output `horizon-window-fold-test.txt`)
Own-data test on 80 random G2 development stars (1,179 star-sectors, real good-time windows of each star-sector's longest-covered product, coverage only so S = 1, eclipse masks not applied):
- **Per star-sector, W = min(1, covered/P) is good:** within 2% of the folded coverage at 43 days and longer (ratio approximate/folded 0.98 to 0.99), 3% at 24 days, but 10% too high at the 13.4-day bin (the folded value is 0.905: a gap at each end of both orbits lets some epochs miss every crossing). CONFIRMED on this sample.
- **The independent-epochs step overstates a star's coverage:** folding all of a star's sectors together gives 0.93 / 0.84 / 0.69 / 0.48 / 0.38 at 43 / 78 / 139 / 250 / 448 days, against 0.985 / 0.91 / 0.77 / 0.60 / 0.44 for 1 - product(1 - W); the approximate-to-folded ratio is 1.00 at 13 to 24 days and 1.06 / 1.08 / 1.12 / 1.24 / 1.15 above. Likely cause (NOT TESTED): a star's sectors repeat nearby phases, so their windows overlap more than independence assumes. So the published ceilings are too low (optimistic) by roughly 5 to 25% at periods of 43 days and longer. This moves the region ceiling of Amendment 2 from 2.1% to about 2.2 to 2.4% (an estimate, not a rerun) and does not change any reading.
- **Not fixed in the calculator:** a correct star-level value needs the folded windows of every star-sector of each set (thousands of file reads, not counted); it is queued as Horizon R1b in the BUILD-QUEUE. Every Horizon number that is shown must carry the sentence "ceilings are optimistic by up to about a quarter at long periods (measured on 80 development stars)".
- **Armstrong 2014 read (abstract, https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.5617):** 10.0 (+18, -6.5)% at 95% limits for R_p > 6 Earth radii and P_p < 300 d, if circumbinary planets are preferentially coplanar; a lower limit of 47% if isotropic; from the seven Kepler circumbinary systems known then. The abstract does not say whether the rate is per binary or per what sample; Kepler eclipsing binaries and TESS ones differ, so how the 10% maps onto our stars is UNCERTAIN (labelled so wherever the comparison is shown).

## Amendment 4 (6 Nov 2026, T138; Ahmed said yes on 6 Nov; written and committed BEFORE any map is re-run with it; no number below was tuned on a result)
Its design was changed once by the independent Opus 5.5 trial audit (`OPUS-TRIAL-AUDIT-2026-11-06.md`): two of its findings, F-1 and F-2, reproduced when I re-ran its scripts, and they replace the 4 Nov probe design. The 4 Nov probes (`census_step1/t138_geometry_probe.py`, `t138_incl_probe.py`) stay on disk as exploratory work and are not used.
1. **Why.** Section 4 treats every coplanar planet of an eclipsing binary as one that crosses a star at each pass. It does not: a planet in the binary's plane crosses star A only for some orientations of that plane, even when the binary itself eclipses. Without this factor the maps are too generous (Q1 note, `Q1-EXPECTED-PLANETS-2026-11-04.md`). Oddo et al. 2026 carry such a factor and Horizon did not.
2. **The rule.** For a star with binary period P_b and a cell with planet period P_p (circular orbits, planet coplanar with the binary, total mass the same for both orbits as in section 5): `g(P_b, P_p) = min(1, (P_b / P_p)^(2/3) / f)`, where f = R_sum / R_A, R_sum being the sum of the two stellar radii and R_A the radius of the crossed star. This is the first-order form R_A / a_p of the coplanar condition (Li, Holman and Tao 2016, eq. 7, read in full) divided by the eclipse window R_sum / a_b, with a_b / a_p = (P_b / P_p)^(2/3); the exact form of Martin and Triaud 2015 (eq. 26) is R_A / (a_p - a_A) and is about 15 to 20% larger at P_p 78 to 140 d. The first-order form is chosen because it matches what one sector sees (the single-conjunction snapshot): the audit's 3D Monte Carlo (3,000 isotropic eclipsing binaries per row, which I re-ran: all 8 rows identical) gives a mean snapshot share of 0.135 at P_p 77.8 d and 0.084 at 139.8 d, against 0.137 and 0.093 first-order and 0.156 and 0.101 exact.
3. **R_sum is not measured for these stars.** f is a scenario: main 1.8, sensitivities 1.5 and 2.2 (the range of the 4 Nov probe, chosen before the audit). The stellar radius R_A cancels, so no star radius, no mass and no eclipse width enters g.
4. **No per-star impact parameter, no eclipse width.** The audit (F-2) showed that a width-based b needs inputs right to about 1% to decide the transit, which they are not (10 to 30% uncertain), and that the 4 Nov probe was mostly counting stars whose width exceeded the physical maximum and was clipped to b = 0.
5. **Where it enters.** The detection chance of a star in a cell (section 7) becomes P_g(i, c) = g(P_b,i, P_p,c) x P(i, c). Everything else is unchanged: grid, stability floor, W x S, the region and the cells of rules A and B, the ceiling formula 3.0 / sum. All four scenarios of Amendment 1 (main F = 5.6, F = 3, F = 6.9, dilution 0.5) are re-run with it, each at f = 1.5, 1.8 and 2.2, for G1, G2 development, G2 sealed and G3. The old numbers (no g) stay on disk and are labelled "no geometry, too generous" wherever they are shown.
6. **Tilt: no factor.** For mutual inclinations of 0 to 3 degrees (the range of the known Kepler systems, Li 2016) the one-sector chance stays within about 10% of the coplanar one (audit Monte Carlo, 0.124 to 0.143 against 0.135 at P_p 77.8 d, and 0.089 to 0.097 against 0.084 at 139.8 d). The 4 Nov tilt rule (UPDATE 2 rows with Delta above 0) counted eventual transits with precession, not a one-sector look, and is withdrawn.
7. **Dilution** stays as in section 5: the main scenario assumes the crossed star gives all the light (generous); the dilution 0.5 scenario is the sensitivity. The audit (F-4) notes that f of 1.8 to 2.2 implies a bright companion, so the main scenario is generous by about 10 to 20% on this count (stated, not changed).
8. **UNCERTAIN or NOT TESTED, said now.** Unequal masses (a_A = a_b / 2 is the largest value for star A, slightly generous); eccentric binaries; precession over spans of many sectors; transits of star B (the audit estimates up to about 20% more at f 2.2, NOT TESTED); isotropic (non-coplanar) planets, which are not in this map; the real f of each star; whether Armstrong 2014's rate applies per binary of our kind (Amendment 3, UNCERTAIN).
