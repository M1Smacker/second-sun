# Census idea 4: has anyone already combined circumbinary dips that fit one orbit? (literature check, night of 4 Nov 2026; research stint RS-011; report only)

Idea 4 in the Census plan: circumbinary transits shift in time and depth because the stars move; judge dips together by whether they fit one possible planet orbit across all sectors, instead of each dip alone. Question: is that already done, and what is left that could be ours? Method: five arXiv API queries (circumbinary with search, timing, single transit, linking, TESS) and the abstracts of the six most relevant hits read in full on their arXiv abstract pages. Nothing beyond abstracts was opened.

## Plain verdict
**The concept is NOT original; published methods already link or stack circumbinary dips along a modelled orbit, on Kepler data. What I could not find is that done on sparse TESS single events across many sectors with an upside-down control. That narrower gap may be ours, but I have not proved it.** Label: LIKELY for "concept already published", UNCERTAIN for "the narrow gap is open" (five queries, abstracts only).

## What the abstracts say (CONFIRMED: read on arXiv)
- **Ofir 2008** (arXiv:0805.1116): CB-BLS, a modified box search for circumbinary planets whose transits are neither periodic nor of one depth.
- **Windemuth, Agol and Carter 2019** (arXiv:1909.07443): corrects large timing and duration variations from the binary's reflex motion using Keplerian models of the binary and planet orbits, then allows extra aperiodicity with the Quasi-periodic Automated Transit Search; recovers Kepler-35 and Kepler-64; Kepler data.
- **Kostov, Welsh and Haghighipour 2020** (arXiv:2008.02756): planets that cross the binary several times in one conjunction give an orbital period estimate (within 10 to 20% for low-eccentricity orbits) from data spanning under 5% of the period; they expect hundreds of such candidates in TESS. This is a single-sector version of the linking idea.
- **Martin and Fabrycky 2021, STANLEY** (arXiv:2101.03186): stacks shallow transits of variable duration and interval using N-body integrations; recovers all known Kepler circumbinary planets including the three of Kepler-47; says it will be applied to a broad sample of eclipsing binaries. This is the closest published match to idea 4, with full dynamics.
- **Davies et al. 2026, mono-cbp** (arXiv:2604.09435): semi-automated search of the TESS Eclipsing Binary Catalogue for individual transit events with automated vetting; one candidate; recovery 50% or more for each known planet, over 75% for 9 of 14. Individual events, not orbit linking (from the abstract).
- **Klagyivik et al. 2016** (arXiv:1610.08471): CoRoT, removal of binary eclipses and a search for quasi-periodic transit-like features, upper limits for coplanar planets.
- Already in our files from earlier work: Oddo et al. 2026 (single-transit detection on TESS M-dwarf binaries, occurrence limits), Armstrong 2014, mono-cbp (see `research/audit-2026-10-28-varwise-mono-cbp.md`).

## What could still be ours (original means a new combination finished properly, not "nobody ever")
1. **Linking across sectors for sparse TESS single events**, with a planet orbit model that predicts where the next dips should fall, tested against many sectors per star. STANLEY and Windemuth do the stacking on continuous Kepler light; mono-cbp and Oddo stop at single events. Not found in five queries (UNCERTAIN).
2. **A real control:** the upside-down copy of every light curve goes through the same linking, so the false-alarm rate of the linking step is measured, not assumed. None of the abstracts mentions such a control (abstracts only; NOT TESTED in the full texts).
3. **Preregistered, on a list nobody has searched** (Census). That is the combination.

## Plan change
- Census stage 5 (research the idea) is downgraded from "maybe novel" to "borrow and extend": read STANLEY and Windemuth in full before designing any linking step; claim nothing as new except the narrow combination above, and only after a second, wider search (Google Scholar style queries, citations of STANLEY and Windemuth, ADS) that I have not done.
- Keep the plain use: two or more dips that agree with one stable circumbinary orbit are better evidence than one, so linking is also a way to bring S2 (false alarms) down, because a chance coincidence of two noise dips on one orbit is rarer. Estimated effect NOT tested.
- Sparse sampling is the catch: a planet at 77 days gives about one transit in three 27-day sectors, so most stars have only one or two events; linking helps only for stars with many sectors, which again says choose stars with long coverage (Q2).

## Addendum (same night): STANLEY and Windemuth read in more detail (arXiv full text, ar5iv; a PDF of STANLEY was fetched to a scratch folder to see its size and deleted unread)
- **STANLEY (Martin and Fabrycky 2021)** says the standard way every Kepler discovery was made, and the only TESS one (TOI-1338), is by eye on individual transits, which "inhibits the discovery of small planets" and makes it "difficult to quantify the efficiency of this method and constrain non-detections" (introduction, quoted in substance). It lists the earlier automated attempts: Jenkins et al. 1996, Ofir 2008 (CB-BLS), Carter and Agol 2013 (QATS), Armstrong et al. 2014, Klagyivik et al. 2017, Windemuth et al. 2019. Its ingredients: bespoke detrending for eclipsing binaries (its Section II.5 treats the transit duration variations as a problem for the high-pass filter), N-body models to stack transits of variable duration and interval, an optimised search grid to keep the cost down, and a Signal Detection Efficiency (SDE) statistic per period. It recovers the known planets and sets detection limits by injecting simulated planets of varying depth. It names TESS revisits of the Kepler field as a place new planets may show. Gaps are in the text: it ends "a prelude to a second paper"; a search for that paper II on arXiv found nothing (NOT TESTED elsewhere).
- **Borrow for Census:** (1) an injection-recovery limit per star, which we already have; (2) a per-period detection statistic (SDE) for the linking step; (3) a binary-specific detrender, which our eclipse mask partly is; (4) the idea that the false-positive signature of aperiodic timing with duration changes is hard to mimic (Kostov et al. 2020). Do not borrow the N-body integration cost unless the linking step proves worth it.
- **Windemuth, Agol and Carter 2019** is a Kepler-only method (Keplerian model corrections plus QATS); applying it to sparse TESS single events is the part not seen in the abstracts.

## Addendum 2 (same night): mono-cbp full text, and Li, Holman and Tao 2016
- **Davies et al. 2026 (mono-cbp), arXiv HTML read:** the sample comes from the TESS Eclipsing Binary Catalogue (Prsa 2022), which confirms the old list is still the working standard; the search is for individual events; the full text says TESS "is more challenging" because of 27-day sectors. For its injection tests it inverted the light curves first so as not to bias them with real signals. I found no step that links events across sectors on one orbit, and no false-alarm control on real data by upside-down copies (text searched for link, same planet, multiple sectors, control; only the detrending context matched). CONFIRMED for what the text says; the absence is NOT TESTED beyond those searches.
- **Li, Holman and Tao 2016 (arXiv:1608.01768, abstract read):** gives an analytical transit probability for eclipsing binaries with a finite observation period and planetary orbital precession; concludes mutual inclinations of the observed transiting systems are nearly coplanar (about 3 degrees or less), possibly implying a circumbinary occurrence rate similar to single stars, and that Lidov-Kozai formation fits the lack of transiting planets around short binaries. This is the "Li 2016 transit probability" left open in the uncertainty register (H2). Use it, together with Martin and Triaud's Eq. 26, in T138: a few degrees of mutual inclination raise g above the exactly-coplanar value of the probe, so the probe's 0.05 to 0.2 is a floor, not a ceiling (LIKELY).
- Net effect on novelty: unchanged (UNCERTAIN). The narrow combination is single TESS events linked across sectors with a real upside-down control, preregistered on an unsearched list.

## Addendum 3 (4 Nov 2026, morning): wider novelty search, three more queries
- **STANLEY follow-up (UNCERTAIN, search snippet only):** Martin and Fabrycky's Paper I (arXiv:2101.03186) says a second paper would apply STANLEY to a broader sample of eclipsing binaries for the size distribution. A 2022 AAS abstract (baas.aas.org/pub/2022n5i207p02, returned HTTP 403 to my script, so NOT opened) is reported to say small planets are less abundant around binaries. No Paper II arXiv number or journal reference found. Still Kepler, still continuous light: it does not close the gap for sparse TESS single events.
- **Dearth of small circumbinary planets (CONFIRMED from the abstract):** Fitzmaurice, Martin and Fabrycky 2022 (arXiv:2202.11719, MNRAS) write of "an observed dearth of small (< 3 Earth radii) circumbinary planets" and propose resonant ejection as a cause. Consequence for us: our completeness is weakest for small dips, and the literature already expects few small planets, so a Census null at small sizes is weak news while a null at Jupiter size (our strongest regime) is the strong news. Plan change: the Census write-up must report the size range it can exclude, not one rate.
- **Thornton et al. 2026** (arXiv:2512.07934, search snippet only, NOT opened, UNCERTAIN): 27 circumbinary candidates from apsidal precession in 1,590 TESS eclipsing binaries from Gaia DR3. A different method (timing of the binary itself, not transits), so a possible cross-check list for candidate stars, not a competitor.
- **Davies 2026 and Oddo 2026:** the new queries returned the same two papers already covered above; no extra citation of a cross-sector linking method turned up. Verdict on novelty unchanged: the narrow sparse-TESS-single-event linking idea with an upside-down control is UNCERTAIN, not found in eight queries in total.
