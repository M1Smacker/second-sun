## 3. Step 1: how small a dip does the whole chain recover?
Method: the same fake dip (trapezoid, 20% ingress and egress) at the same ppm and time in every file of a star-sector, then the unchanged frozen code. 299 random star-sectors (of 5,082), 4 box lengths, 6 depths, dip and mirror bump, 14,352 injections. CONFIRMED that the harness is faithful: with nothing injected it reproduces the real events (kind, product, centre, SNR, tier) in 299 of 299 star-sectors, and `chain_recovery_test.py` passes with two mutations failing as they should.
- **A ceiling far below 100%.** At 18 times the 8 h noise the dip is seen at SNR 7 in 98 to 99% of cases but ends in tier 1 in only 62% (4 h), 64% (8 h), 51% (14 h) and 44% (20 h). Long dips fare worst.
- **Half recovered needs about 11 times the 8 h noise** (10.9 for 8 h boxes, 13.1 for 4 h, 13.2 for 14 h); 90% is never reached. For the median star (8 h noise 746 ppm) that is about 8,000 ppm; for the brightest, quietest class (312 ppm) about 3,100 ppm.
- **Where dips are lost (K 12 or more, dips that are primary events, n = 6,704):** tier 1 55%, tier 2 44%. Flags that look at data edges (gap and dump, 1 day ramp, flat baseline) remove 32% of the dips; eclipse overlap, eclipse phase, common moment, repeater and dominance remove 12% more. The flat baseline (step) flag grows with length: 10% of 4 and 8 h dips, 25% of 14 h, 37% of 20 h, and 47% of the step-flagged tier 2 dips carry no gap or ramp flag. LIKELY: the 2 day detrending window absorbs part of a long dip (FIX-QUEUE 11); NOT TESTED.
- **The SAP twin adds nothing for fake planet-like dips:** of the tier-1 recoveries, 77% are "PDCSAP only", 22% "both", 0% "SAP only". Seven of the 21 real tier-1 dips are "SAP only". LIKELY a sign they are unlike planets as injected, but not proof: on TOI-1338 b the raw light caught transits PDC missed.

## 4. Planet size: depth first, radius only as a range
- The radius columns of `chain_recovery_v7.txt` assume the crossed star has the TIC radius and carries all or half the light. G1 has neither number. For the median star at 50% recovery: 21 to 30 Earth radii if the crossed star is 2.18 Sun radii (the median TIC radius), 10 to 14 if it is 1.0 Sun radius. TIC radii of binaries describe the blended point, so the true range is wide. UNCERTAIN.
- Known circumbinary planets, from the NASA Exoplanet Archive (pscomppars, cb_flag 1, queried 29 Oct, 53 rows; 34 have planet and host radius). Single star depth (planet radius over host radius, squared, host carries all the light, so an UPPER bound): Kepler-16 b 14,250 ppm; Kepler-1660 b 6,292; TIC 172900988 b 5,552; Kepler-35 b 5,292; Kepler-47 d 4,755; Kepler-453 b 4,662; Kepler-34 b 4,567; Kepler-1647 b 3,697; TOI-1338 b 2,861; Kepler-413 b 2,637; Kepler-1661 b 2,168; Kepler-47 c 2,074; Kepler-64 b 1,068; Kepler-47 b 892; Kepler-38 b 506. (The archive flag also holds non-transiting companions; I kept only the transiting Kepler and TESS ones.)
- So against 8,000 ppm only Kepler-16 b clears the 50% depth for the median star; against the best class (about 3,100 ppm) roughly 8 of those 15 do. LIKELY consequence: a null on G1 excludes giant planets and quiet bright stars, NOT typical circumbinary planets. That is still the clean measured null Ahmed asked for, but a weak one, and it must be said that way.

## 5. Mirror bump: do the flags treat a dip and its mirror alike? (the assumption behind report line 5b)
- Paired tier 1 recovery, 3,588 pairs at K 8, 10 and 12: dip 32%, bump 34%, exact p = 0.06 (K 10 alone: 35% against 38%, p = 0.09; K 12: 47% against 47%). Per flag the shares are within about 1 point (step 21.5% against 20.4%, gap and dump 16.4% against 16.6%, common moment 11.0% against 10.7%); dominance is 4.0% for dips against 2.5% for bumps. LIKELY: the flags are close to neutral for planet-like dips, with a small edge for the upside-down side (about 2 points). The size of the noise asymmetry (1.25 to 2.5 times) is a separate effect and is not touched by this test. Cause of the small edge UNCERTAIN.

## 6. What this changes
1. The top scientific question moves from "how small" to "which flags cost recovery without buying enough protection". Cost per flag is now known (section 3); the benefit per flag (false alarms removed in the upside-down copy) is in the aggregates; putting them side by side is the "judge" of the plan (step 4). Candidate v8 gains: longer detrending window for long boxes, a flat-baseline test that allows for the detrending, and a gap rule that scales with box length.
2. G2 needs about 28 GB of downloads (coverage query, see `g2-counts-2026-10-29.md`), not 1 to 4 GB as I first guessed.
3. Limits of this run: 6% of the star-sectors; one dip per run; random cadence; trapezoid shape; depth grid stops at 18 times the noise, so a 90% level cannot be located; placement avoids existing events (a small bias); the common moment pool is the real run's events only.
