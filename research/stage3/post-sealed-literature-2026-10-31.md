## Answered
**Q1. TOI-1338 b in the later sectors: CONFIRMED, and it changes the sensitivity story.**
- Our preregistered epochs are exactly the 11 rows of Kostov et al. 2020 Table 6 ([arXiv:2004.07783](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07783)). Wang and Liu 2024 ([arXiv:2404.18415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18415)) say the observed transits "offset from the predicted transit ephemerides ... which assumed a one-planet model, by 0.5-1 transit durations" (perturbation by TOI-1338 c is their suggestion). Their Table 1 gives the observed mid-times; observed minus Kostov: S27 +0.259 d, S34 +0.303, S37 +0.016, S62 +0.282, S65 +0.608, S68 +0.306. Kostov's own quoted uncertainty for the 2025 transits was 0.14 to 0.22 d, the real offsets are 0.83 to 1.31 d.
- Our events against the later times (window 3 sigma + 0.1 d, script `q1_toi1338_refit.py`): 7 of the 10 sectors with an updated time hold a real event within 1.7 hours of it (S27, S37, S65, S89, S93, S96, S98); none holds an upside-down one. Chance probability under uniform events 5e-10. Sectors 89, 93, 96, 98 were forecast in Wang and Liu's Table 3 on 29 Apr 2024, before those sectors were observed: 4 of 4 hit, chance 6e-7 (an out-of-sample test). Event depths 2,000 to 3,500 ppm, matching the roughly 0.2% depth in Kostov.
- Fate of the 10 transits: tier 1 in 4 (S37 1L, S65 1A, S89 1A, S93 1A); found but sent to tier 2 in 3 (S27 ramp; S96 gap-dump + step + dominated; S98 dominated alone); not found in 3 (S34, S62, S68: no event of either kind within 1.5 d). S30 has no updated time (their fit excluded it: damaged egress).
- The preregistered S6 line stays 1 of 7 (written before the run). The post hoc line is: found at some tier in 7 of 10, kept at tier 1 in 4 of 10 (40%, planted-dip tier-1 completeness at the matching K of 7 is 20%, same order).
- Plan changes: T44 text ready; the sensitivity statement is "about 4 to 7 of 10, not 1 of 7"; any future known-planet test must use a forecast that carries model uncertainty (photodynamical fit), with the window written before the run; new T102 (loss ledger per transit).

**Q3. Are the short deep upside-down survivors flares? Shape CONFIRMED, cause LIKELY.**
- Measured on the raw fast-cadence light curves (script `q3_flare_shape.py`), asymmetry = late third minus early third of the box, in units of box depth, in the search's orientation; a flare read upside down starts abruptly and recovers slowly (positive). Tier-1 upside-down (42 measured of 59; 17 are slow-cadence and not measurable): median +0.52, half above 0.5. Tier-1 real (26): median -0.04, 12% above 0.5. Other real events with good coverage: 14%; other upside-down: 20%. One-sided Mann-Whitney p = 0.0001 (flip tier-1 against real tier-1).
- Threshold table (flip tier-1 / real tier-1 / other real / other flip above): 1.0: 43% / 4% / 9% / 10%; 1.5: 36% / 4% / 5% / 7%; 2.0: 24% / 0% / 2% / 2%. TOI-1338 b events measured: -0.09, +0.13, -0.02 (box-like).
- The flare template (sharp rise, exponential decay) is Davenport 2014 ([arXiv:1411.3723](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.3723)); I saw it only in a search result, so that part is UNCERTAIN until the paper is opened. No flare-catalogue cross-match was done.
- Plan changes: T33 becomes a concrete switch (flag asymmetry above 1.0): on the sealed run it would remove about 18 of the 42 measured tier-1 upside-down events and 1 of 26 real ones. Its price on planted box dips is NOT TESTED (planted boxes are symmetric by construction; the empirical rate on other events is 9% to 10%).

**Q4. Unit and estimate for the control: CONFIRMED (own data), literature LIKELY.**
- Star-level cluster bootstrap with the ratio re-estimated inside every draw (script `q4_star_unit.py`). The variance is inflated by the clustering (design effect): sealed 2.6, 2.5, 1.9, 1.9 at bars 7, 8, 10, 12; development with c6 1.2 to 2.0; development under 0.7.1 2.2 to 5.0. Under 0.7.1 the event-level Poisson p was 1.4e-19 against 0.1% of star-bootstrap draws.
- The asymmetry ratio (upside-down per real raw event, whole run): sealed 1.18 [95% 1.01 to 1.40] at bar 7, 1.28, 1.39, 1.54 [1.17 to 2.07] at bars 8, 10, 12; development 0.92 [0.70 to 1.21], then about 1.0. At bar 7 the two slices' intervals overlap: the slice difference is within the noise of the estimate.
- Heavy stars drag the ratio: dropping the 25 heaviest stars (by raw events) gives 1.36 (sealed) and 1.33 (development), almost equal.
- Sealed real minus expected (full-run ratio): bar 7 -23.8 [-51.0, +1.0]; bar 8 -17.6 [-40.2, +3.2]; bar 10 -18.2 [-32.9, -5.7]; bar 12 -10.6 [-21.8, -0.9]. Real light keeps FEWER dips than the control predicts, significantly at bars 10 and 12. The cause is UNCERTAIN (real events carry more eclipse-related flags: c5 13% against 7%, eclipse overlap 8% against 4%, flip match 2.5% against 0%).
- Two different numbers must be printed: the upper limit given what we saw (+1 to +8 kept dips excess excluded, depending on the ratio) and the detectable excess at 80% power (sealed 33, 27, 17, 13 kept dips at bars 7, 8, 10, 12; development 26, 18, 13, 11).
- Source: Kepler DR25 ([Thompson et al. 2018, arXiv:1710.06758](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.06758)) states the inversion control is not perfectly symmetric ("not true" for shape and frequency), that the pipeline removes inversion false alarms less well than scrambling ones, uses the union of inverted and scrambled sets, and finds the two sets differ by up to 40% in some period ranges; a random half of the pooled set changed reliability with a standard deviation of 8 points. The parts I could read say nothing about several false alarms on one star.
- Plan changes: T38 (star as the unit), T39 (ratio per bar with interval, from the whole run), new T101 (add a scrambled control beside the upside-down one, judged on the pooled union), and the scorecard prints both the limit and the detectable excess.

**Q5. What the step check measures: CONFIRMED (own data); the fix is UNCERTAIN.**
- Components on all raw events (SNR 7 or more): "less than half the 6 h next to the event has data" on 63% to 80%; no lift measurable on a side 46% to 60%; lift above half the depth on 34% to 53%; step fires on 75% to 96% depending on box and kind; firing does not depend on SNR (88%, 89%, 83%, 84%). The median of the smaller side cover is 0.00 to 0.25: most raw events sit beside a data gap. So most step firing is the coverage rule catching edge events, which is what it was built for.
- Price of each check (script `q5_checks_price.py`; drop one check alone, count planted dips that move to tier 1 and sealed events that come with them): step +9.8 / +10.1 points (sealed / development) for +94 real and +68 upside-down events (6.8 upside-down per point); gap-dump +4.3 / +4.9 for +13 real, +8 upside-down (1.7 per point); repeater +1.9 / +2.8 for +23, +10 (4.3); dominated +2.5 / +1.6 for +7, +4 (2.0); ramp +2.0 / +2.4 for +5, +3 (1.4); c5 +0.8 / +0.7 for +11 real and 0 upside-down; eclipse overlap +0.3 / +1.1 for +3 and 0; flip match and common moment about 0.
- One false alarm per 1,000 star-sectors is 6.7 upside-down events in 6,734 star-sectors. The step check costs about 1.0 per point (matching the earlier C-study); gap-dump, ramp and dominated together buy about 8.9 points (if additive, NOT TESTED together) for 15 upside-down events (0.25 per point). Relaxing them alone would lift S2 from 8.8 to about 11 per 1,000 (mark 8); the flare switch of Q3 would take away about 18 events (-2.7 per 1,000): a candidate pairing, NOT TESTED.
- Not known: how many of the lost planted dips lay beside a gap (the planted-dip table stores no lift or cover). Plan changes: T34 becomes two switches (coverage rule, lift rule) priced separately; new T103 (price table in every scorecard); the planted table records cover and lift next time.

**Q2. What a null of this power rules out: LIKELY (arithmetic on own data), comparison with the literature UNCERTAIN.**
- Noise per 8 h box 329 ppm (median); tier-1 completeness against K (8 h box): 12% at K 6, 28% at 8, 47% at 10, 57% at 12, 59% at 14 (script `q2_null_power.py`, `q2b_q6_q12.py`). The stars have 11.3 sectors on average (median 6), about 26 usable days each. For a planet of period P, one sector catches a transit with probability about 26/P, so a planet-bearing star gives on average (sectors x 26 / P) x completeness tier-1 detections over all its sectors.
- For a TOI-1338 b-like planet (depth 2,300 ppm, K 7, completeness 20%): 0.99 / 0.62 / 0.40 / 0.20 detections per planet-bearing star at P = 60 / 95 / 150 / 300 d. A 4 Earth-radii planet (1,000 ppm): 0.19 at 95 d. A 2 Earth-radii planet: nothing.
- Upper limit on (planets per binary) x (geometry factor g, the chance that such a planet transits): N_ul / (594 x detections per star), N_ul = 8.3 (preregistered-ratio bound) or 1.0 (full-run ratio bound). TOI-1338 b-like, 95 d: 2.2% or 0.27%; 300 d: 7.1% or 0.85%. 4 Earth radii, 95 d: 7.4% or 0.9%.
- Armstrong et al. 2014 ([arXiv:1404.5617](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.5617)): occurrence of planets with R > 6 Earth radii and P < 300 d, near-coplanar, among Kepler binaries, "10.0 (+18, -6.5)%" (95% limits), "47%" lower limit if the orbits are isotropic; 1,735 Kepler eclipsing binaries, 308 usable; they give no transit-probability formula (their Monte Carlo handles it). So our numbers cannot be set against the 10% until g for eclipsing binaries and our hosts' radii are known (several leads have TIC radii of 2 to 5 solar radii, which make a planet's transit shallower; two have no radius). Conclusion allowed today: the null excludes nothing about planets under 4 Earth radii or periods near 300 d, and it cannot be called a contradiction of the Kepler rate. It does not contradict it either. Claim "no giant planets here" stays forbidden.
- Plan changes: new T105 (efficiency table beside every null), Q6 matters more (single sectors catch 27% of 95-day transits).

## What this changes in Second Sun (short)
1. The known-planet test was weaker than the search: re-scored with the later published times, Vesper finds TOI-1338 b in 7 of 10 transits, 4 at tier 1, and predicted four of them blind.
2. The control's survivors are flare-shaped; a shape flag is a concrete, cheap next switch.
3. Every safety check now has a price; three cheap ones can be relaxed if the flare switch pays for them (a hypothesis, not tested).
4. The scorecard needs the star as the unit, a per-bar ratio with an interval, the detectable excess beside the limit, and a second (scrambled) control.
5. Single sectors catch a quarter of 95-day transits, so linking sectors is the biggest power gain and the null cannot yet say "no planets".
6. A candidate can only reach ExoFOP through a paper or a collaborator.
