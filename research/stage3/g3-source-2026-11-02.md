# G3 star list: the candidate source found on 2 Nov 2026 (T111a)

Question: after sector 26, where do binaries that our saved Prsa 2022 table lacks come from? Answer from the primary pages (read by the web-fetch helper, quotes verbatim there; labels below).

**Best candidate: the TESS Ten Thousand Catalog.** Kostov et al. 2025, ApJS 279, 50 ([arXiv:2506.05631](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05631), [article data page](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/1538-4365/ade2d8/data)). 10,001 vetted eclipsing binaries from TESS full-frame images: 7,936 new and 2,065 known systems with corrected periods (CONFIRMED, abstract and summary). Licence: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 for the article data (CONFIRMED, article data page).

**Files (plain text machine-readable tables, sizes not shown; my estimate for table 3 is about 1 to 2 MB, UNCERTAIN):**
- Table 3, the 7,936 new EBs: `https://content.cld.iop.org/journals/0067-0049/279/2/50/revision1/apjsade2d8t3_mrt.txt`
- Table 4, the 2,065 known EBs with TESS, Gaia, ASAS-SN, ATLAS, VSX and WISE periods: `.../apjsade2d8t4_mrt.txt`
- Table 2 (about 900,000 unvetted targets) is not wanted.
Neither file has been fetched; reachability is unverified. Needs Ahmed's yes before any download.

**Columns of table 3 (CONFIRMED from the paper's table header):** TIC ID, RA, Dec, Tmag, Period, T0 primary, depth and duration of the primary, T0 secondary, phase, depth and duration of the secondary, Sectors, RUWE, AEN, AENS, Teff, Comments. There is NO morphology column, so "detached" must be judged from depth and eclipse duration if we want it.

**What it means for the G3 rule:**
- The stars are faint: mean, median and 95th percentile Tmag 13.6, 13.8, 14.9 (CONFIRMED, paper text). Our G2 rule used Tmag 13.5 or brighter, where Vesper's floor is already about 6 Earth radii at Tmag 11.5 (earlier measurement). Most of this catalogue is fainter than our rule.
- Periods: mean 4.5 d, median 3.5 d, 95th percentile 11.6 d, longest about 40 d (CONFIRMED). The count with P over 7 days is not stated. My rough guess for "P over 7 d and Tmag 13.5 or brighter" is 100 to 500 stars (UNCERTAIN, from the percentiles only, not a count). It could be too few for a useful group; the real count comes only from the table.
- Sector range is stated two ways: Sectors 1 to 82 in the abstract and "1 through 26" in the summary (UNCERTAIN, the paper contradicts itself).
- No completeness or false-positive rate is given for the vetted 10,001 (CONFIRMED that it is not stated). Ephemerides may be slightly off for shallow, few-eclipse targets (paper's own caveat).
- Overlap with the 4,584-star Prsa table is not stated; we compute it from table 3.
- The paper reports no new circumbinary planet; it notes many of the new EBs probably sit in multiple systems (RUWE above 1.4 for about one in four).

**Other pool (not looked at yet):** Gaia DR3 eclipsing-binary candidates, 2,184,477 sources ([Mowlavi et al. 2023](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A%26A...674A..16M/abstract)). Joining to TESS needs the TIC to Gaia DR3 cross-match table from ExoFOP (a larger download). Heavier than table 3; only if table 3 gives too few stars.

## Update 2 Nov 2026 (Ahmed said yes to the two tables; downloaded 04:5x)
Both URLs answered 200: table 3 is 2,002,473 bytes and table 4 is 309,972 bytes (`data/g3/catalog_ttc`, hashes in MANIFEST.txt). CONFIRMED counts (`g3-pool-counts-2026-11-02.txt`): table 3 has 7,936 EBs with no overlap with G1, G2 or the saved Prsa table; P over 7 d and Tmag 13.5 or brighter = 612 stars (my guess of 100 to 500 was a little low); 451 of them have RUWE 1.4 or less; 605 have 2 or more sectors. Caveat: these stars are faint (median Tmag 12.8 for the 612), so most of their star-sectors will have only QLP or TESS-SPOC light, and QLP-only groups depend on the manifest (FIX-QUEUE item 70). No rule is locked.
