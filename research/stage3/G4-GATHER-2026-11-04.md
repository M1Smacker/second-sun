## 1. Why G3 was fast
Short answer: **the search was never the slow part, and G3 had much less to read. Vesper did not get faster.**
- **Same rules, same code (CONFIRMED).** Vesper 0.8.1 gives the same events as 0.8.0 on 300 of 300 star-sectors of G1 and of the sealed G2 (the zero-difference proofs). Nothing in the search became quicker; only the data paths changed.
- **What took the hours (CONFIRMED, `research/jobs.md`, `data\sealed_steps_log.txt`).** G2 sealed: download of 17,383 files (14.6 GB) from about 17:20 to 18:3x; preparation about 1 hour; the search 19:28 to 20:52 (1 h 24 min); asymmetry 6.5 min; completeness 23 min; scorecard 10 s. G3: download from 2 Nov 05:21 to 3 Nov 02:40, about 21 hours with restarts after MAST time-outs (CONFIRMED, download logs); the files on disk are 2.2 GB for G3 against 14.6 GB for the G2 sealed stars alone (23 GB for all of G2; G1 5.5 GB); then preparation and proofs; the search itself 4 min 43 s.
- **Even G1 searches were short (CONFIRMED, file times in `data\stage3`).** Each G1 run of 5,082 star-sectors wrote its files in 5 to 10 minutes. The G2 development run (3,425 star-sectors) took about 9 minutes.
- **G3 read 5.3 times less light (CONFIRMED, `audit_all.csv`).**

| | G2 sealed | G3 |
|---|---|---|
| files | 17,383 | 5,031 |
| star-sectors searched | 6,877 | 4,080 |
| cadences loaded | 150.3 million | 28.2 million |
| 2-minute SPOC files | 5,332 | 126 |
| QLP files | 6,775 | 4,075 |
| SAP groups (second pass) | 4,233 | 513 |

  G3's stars are faint, so almost none is on the 2-minute target list; its light is mostly 10 to 30 minute QLP files. 150.3 / 28.2 = 5.3 and 7,341 / 4,399 = 1.7, so about 9 times less work; the observed ratio is 17.8, leaving about 2 times from the machine (an idle PC, 8 workers at Normal priority, no one using it, against 6 workers at BelowNormal while he used the PC on 30 Oct). That last 2 times is LIKELY, not measured.
- **Not a sign of a skipped job (CONFIRMED, S8):** all 4,080 star-sectors were read. One real limit: 784 of 5,634 product reads (13.9%) had "no safe windows" and were not searched.
- Counts he remembered: G1 549 stars (Kepler-field binaries), G2 895 stars (594 sealed + 301 development), G3 612 listed and 611 searched.

## 2. What is unsearched (the answer is a population, not a patch of sky)
- **The sky itself is not the gap (CONFIRMED, ecliptic latitude of every catalogue row).** Our searched stars mirror their parent catalogues: about 42% north of the ecliptic, 4 to 6% within 12 degrees of the ecliptic plane, 31 to 43% above 54 degrees (TESS's long-coverage zones). The unsearched remainder has the same spread. There is no empty region to go and fill.
- **What is left, counted from the tables (CONFIRMED, read-only scripts on `data/g3/catalog_ttc` and `data/xmatch/ebs.csv`).**

| Pool | Stars | Why not searched | Prior for a circumbinary planet |
|---|---|---|---|
| TTC new EBs, period over 7 d, Tmag 13.5 to 15.5 | 785 (548 + 237) | too faint for the G3 rule | same as G3, but fainter: weaker |
| TTC new EBs, period 5 to 7 d, Tmag 13.5 or brighter | 495 | G3 rule asked for over 7 d | low: no Kepler transiting planet below 7 d (Hamers et al. 2016) |
| TTC new EBs, period 2 to 5 d, bright | 1,205 | same | very low |
| TTC new EBs, period under 2 d, bright | 634 | same | very low |

| EBs found in 2-minute data after sector 26 | unknown | no public list: MAST TESS-EBs is v1.0, sectors 1 to 26, updated 2022-06-29 (CONFIRMED on the page) | unknown size |
| Gaia DR3 eclipsing-binary candidates | 2,184,477 (CONFIRMED, Mowlavi et al. 2023 abstract; periods over 0.2 d; completeness 25 to 50% against OGLE) | never cross-matched to TESS | unknown; how many are bright, detached and over 7 d is NOT TESTED |

  Totals: 2,056 distinct stars searched in G1 + G2 + G3; 19,464 star-sectors (G1 5,082; G2 dev 3,425 and sealed 6,877; G3 4,080).
- **The 7-day cut has a source (CONFIRMED, abstract read):** Hamers, Perets and Portegies Zwart 2016 (arXiv:1506.02039) state that Kepler found no transiting circumbinary planet around a binary shorter than 7 days and give a triple-star explanation. The figure of about 7.4 d for the shortest known host, Kepler-47, comes from a search summary of that paper, not from the abstract (LIKELY).

## 3. Rabtsevich's 100,000 (CONFIRMED, our RS-007 notes and his Note)
He searched **Sector 98 QLP light curves of 126,246 stars (Tmag up to 11.5), box least squares, then LEO-Vetter**. That is ONE sector, all bright stars, ordinary planets, no eclipsing-binary mask. In our unit that is 126,246 star-sectors, 6.5 times all our 19,464. His candidate was examined post hoc, below his own shortlist threshold (statistics 10.6 and 11.0 against 15), and is unconfirmed; the new TESS data (Sector 110, 31 Oct to 26 Nov 2026) decide it. We cannot copy his scale: circumbinary planets need eclipsing binaries, a small part of all stars (the share is NOT measured here; the TTC lists 10,001 in FFI data), and the catalogued bright long-period ones are nearly all searched. His stars are bright, so his noise is low; our G3 median noise is 572 ppm per 8 h box.

## 4. What counts is not the number of stars (CONFIRMED, `horizon-first-reach-2026-11-03.md`)
- Sealed G2 (594 stars) is worth about **87 star-equivalents** for a Kepler-16-size planet (7 Earth radii, 77 to 139 d): zero found means a 95% ceiling of about 3.4% of stars for that one cell.
- G3 (612 faint stars, provisional): the same cell reads 15 to 17%, about **18 to 20 star-equivalents**. Six hundred faint stars bought about a fifth of what 594 bright ones bought. For 11 Earth radii G3 reads 4 to 5%.
- So "more stars" is the wrong unit. The unit is detection chance summed over stars. A sample of 20 bright stars with 2-minute data can beat 600 faint ones. The expected number of planets in any hunt is NOT computed (the integration over radius and period cells is not done; the 10% of Armstrong 2014 covers all radii above 6 Earth radii inside 300 d and cannot be used cell by cell). UNCERTAIN.
- Comparison: Oddo et al. 2026 (arXiv:2610.22625, abstract read) searched TESS M-dwarf binaries and set a 95% ceiling of 33.0% (coplanar) for planets of 2 to 16 Earth radii out to 600 d, with one new candidate. Our single-cell ceilings are not the same quantity, so they do not compare directly.

## 6. Options for G4 (not recommended yet)
- **A. Do nothing new for G4 until Q1 and Q2 are answered.** Cheap, no download, and it says whether any pool is worth its hours. My pick.
