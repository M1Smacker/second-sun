## What it means (LIKELY)
- The score leans mostly on the second-product agreement (weight +1.2 to +1.5) and penalises side lifts, depth and long boxes. Those features do not separate held planted dips from background dips: it releases as many planets as it newly holds.
- It favours the upside-down arm because the real-arm background is full of dip-only artefacts that look like planted dips; the upside-down twin has none. This is the same wall as RS-024 and RS-025: near steps, gaps and dumps, false dips outnumber false bumps, so any release there is paid in real alarms the upside-down meter cannot see.
- So RS-039's estimate for "one calibrated score" (+4 to +8 P) was too high with today's features. The held pool (16.7 points) needs a new kind of evidence (pixel-level centroid or per-pixel tests, as the TOI-1338 b team used, RS-038), not a better combination of the same numbers.
