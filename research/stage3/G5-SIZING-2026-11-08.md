# How big could G5 be after Census (G4)? (8 Nov 2026; Ahmed's question; arithmetic on earlier outputs plus two Gaia count queries; LIKELY at best)

## Plain verdict
5,000 stars: yes. 10,000: yes if we reach down to about G 14 to 15. 20,000: only by going faint (G 15 to 16), where each star is worth about a fifth of a bright one. The limit is not downloads (under 100 GB for 10,000 stars) and not the star count: it is how many suitable binaries exist and how many first-tier dips a human can sort.

## The pool (Gaia DR3 eclipsing binaries with period over 5 days)
- CONFIRMED by count query: 2,184,477 eclipsing binaries in `gaiadr3.vari_eclipsing_binary`; 188,407 with a period over 5 d, 137,637 over 7 d (all magnitudes, most faint).
- UNCERTAIN sample: a join with `gaia_source` times out for the whole sky, so I counted 12 of 768 level-3 HEALPix pixels (1.6% of the sky, all in the northern cap, ids chosen only by source_id range) and scaled by 64. Poisson noise alone is about 7% at G 14, but the sky is not uniform, so treat every number as good to a factor of 1.5 to 2. All-sky estimate, period over 5 d: brighter than G 13 about 5,500 (about 2,400 with the plain two-Gaussian model, a detached-like proxy); G 14 about 11,600 (5,500); G 15 about 20,800 (9,900); G 16 about 37,600 (20,700).
- Gaia misses slow binaries (Ahmed, 8 Nov), so these are floors. CALNet (10,531 stars; Gaia period for 15% of them), the TESS Ten Thousand catalogue and Prsa add stars Gaia lacks. TESS must also have observed the star.

## Value per star, main scenario (R0 Amendment 4; f 1.5 to 2.2; E = expected planets at a 10% rate)
- A bright Census-type star (3 sectors, 2-minute data) is worth 0.45 of a G2-sealed star (q2-pool-value.txt). Per 1,000 such stars E is 0.50 (f 2.2 A), 0.62 (f 1.8 A), 1.14 (f 1.5 B).
- A faint star (G 13.5 to 15.5, QLP) is worth about 0.21 of a G2-sealed star by region sum (q2-pool-value.txt line A; UNCERTAIN, the K16 cell gives a similar ratio). Per 1,000 faint stars E is 0.23, 0.29, 0.53.
- After G4 with 1,400 bright stars: E_total of the earlier hunts plus Census is 1.98 (f 2.2 A), 2.43 (f 1.8 A), 4.48 (f 1.5 B).

## G5 size against ceiling and forward odds (mid case f 1.8 A; completeness trim 0.9; G1 to G4 all empty)
| G5 | Added E | 95% ceiling if G4 and G5 are empty | Chance G5 finds at least one (Kepler-like prior / broad prior) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5,000 bright | 3.1 | 5.4% | 75% / 51% |
| 10,000 bright | 6.2 | 3.5% | 91% / 72% |
| 20,000 bright (does not exist) | 12.3 | 2.0% | 98% / 89% |
| 5,000 faint | 1.4 | 7.8% | 51% / 30% |
| 10,000 faint | 2.9 | 5.7% | 73% / 49% |
| 20,000 faint | 5.7 | 3.7% | 90% / 69% |
A realistic G5 of 10,000 stars (half bright, half faint) adds E about 4.5: ceiling about 4.3% if empty, chance of finding at least one about 60% to 85%. Low case (f 2.2 A) is roughly 40% lower in E, high case (f 1.5 B) about 1.8 times higher. For comparison: after G4 alone (1,400 bright stars) the ceiling is 12.3% (mid) and the chance G4 finds one is 21% to 52%. These are chances that a planet is in the data and passes the frozen search, not chances of a confirmed planet. Only giants (above about 6 Earth radii) are in reach.

## The real bottleneck: sorting
The preregistered mark is at most 8 upside-down first-tier dips per 1,000 star-sectors (S2 pooled 8.57). A 10,000-star G5 with about 4 sectors per star is about 40,000 star-sectors: roughly 340 first-tier dips on the upside-down arm and a similar pile of look-alikes on the real arm. That is about ten times what G2 and G3 produced. G5 only makes sense with an automatic vetter trained on labelled events (the Petrova dataset, WO-22, T151) fed from the Annex (T150), so the human sees only survivors of two keys.

## Data volume (disk per star-sector from q2-pool-value.txt)
Bright 2-minute 2.12 MB, faint QLP 0.54 MB. 5,000 bright stars at 3 sectors is about 32 GB; 5,000 faint at 5 sectors about 13 GB. A 10,000-star G5 is about 45 GB; 20,000 faint stars about 55 GB. Fetch speed (about 3 files a second) puts it at hours, not days.

## Not tested
Faint-star value (0.21) rests on one G3 comparison; period dependence of value; TESS coverage of the Gaia stars; whether QLP light curves exist for all G 15 stars; the floor-3 and dilution scenarios; the vetting load of the real arm.
