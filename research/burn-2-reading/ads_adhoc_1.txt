  2026-08  eprint    Littlefield, Colin et al. | The sensitivity of TESS to transiting planets in TOIs with close-in stellar companions | https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260813527L arXiv:2608.13527
     High resolution imaging with optical speckle interferometry has revealed that many transiting exoplanet host stars possess close-in stellar companions. The objective of this study is to quantify how the presence of these companions impacts the ability of TESS to detect the transits of small planets. We accomplish this by examining 2052 TESS Objects of Interest (TOIs) that appear to be single-star systems based on speckle interferometric observations as well as 188 TOIs in unresolved ($&lt; 1.2\arcsec$) stellar binaries. For each planet, we take its transit signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), radius, and orbital period from the TOI catalog and, for planets in stellar binaries, we correct the radius for dilution by the companion. By applying a scaling relation to the measured transit SNR of each TOI in our sample, we determine the detectability of transits in each TOI as a function of both planet radius and orbital period. When applied to the full sample, this procedure elucidates the sensitivity of TESS to transiting planets as a function of binarity, host-star spectral type, planet radius, and planet orbital period. These sensitivity grids quantify the bias against the detection of small planets in unresolved binaries by TESS and show that there is a particularly low sensitivity to planets transiting secondary stars in unresolved binaries, especially as the magnitude difference between the stars increases. These sensitivity grids are available for download to facilitate their use in other studies.
  2026-05  inproceedings Thornton, Margo et al. | Detecting Circumbinary Planets via Apsidal Precession in Eclipsing Binaries | https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026csss.confE..77T 
     Most circumbinary planets have been discovered by their transits, limiting our understanding of such systems to those with mutually coplanar architectures. This bias makes it difficult to infer the true circumbinary planet population, highlighting the need for alternative detection methods that do not rely on transits. To address this, we explore apsidal precession in eclipsing binaries as a dynamical signature of planetary companions. Using TESS photometry for a large sample of eclipsing binaries drawn from the Gaia DR3 catalogue, we identify systems exhibiting measurable apsidal precession that cannot be explained by general relativistic, tidal, or rotational effects alone. These excess precession signals point to the presence of additional gravitational perturbers and allow constraints to be placed on the masses and orbital separations of potential companions. I will present a new sample of circumbinary planet candidates identified through this dynamical approach, along with several higher-mass companion candidates. Although the inferred companion properties remain degenerate, this work demonstrates the promise of apsidal precession as a tool for uncovering potentially misaligned circumbinary systems. I will highlight our ongoing efforts to incorporate radial velocity constraints and extend this method to upcoming data releases (e.g. Gaia DR4), helping to reduce these degeneracies and move toward a complete census of circumbinary companions.

     Circumbinary planets and brown dwarfs provide important tests for planet formation and dynamical stability in multi-star environments, yet they remain rare among known exoplanet populations. Among the available techniques, eclipse timing variations (ETVs) offer a unique and complementary approach to transits and radial velocities, particularly sensitive to massive and long-period companions. TESS enables ETV studies with its nearly all-sky coverage and multi-year temporal baseline, providing extensive photometric monitoring of eclipsing binaries with variable sector coverage. To evaluate the capabilities of the ETV method with TESS, our previous work quantified the detectability of circumbinary companions, finding recovery rates that rise from below 0.1% for planets of 1─5 Jupiter masses, representative of the core-accretion regime, to about 0.3% for 5─13 Jupiter-mass companions, approximately 4% for brown dwarfs between 13 and 60 Jupiter masses, and 8% for more massive brown dwarfs above 60 Jupiter masses, near or beyond the lithium-burning threshold. Building on these results, we expand the survey to include a majority of the TESS eclipsing binaries, predominantly detached systems, covering all TESS sectors released to date. Using TESS light curves from both pipeline and custom full-frame-image extractions, we measure precise mid-times for primary and secondary eclipses (with median timing errors below one minute for about 15% of targets and below ten minutes for about 45%) and search for cyclic ETVs through Lomb─Scargle analyses to identify significant periodic signals and their possible origins. The survey identifies several thousand systems with significant ETV periodicities, including over 2,000 targets exhibiting false-alarm probabilities below one per million. In addition to searching for circumbinary planets and brown dwarfs, we identify ETVs arising from other mechanisms such as stellar companions, apsidal motion, and magnetic activity cycles. We determine the survey completeness with injection-recovery tests across the parameter space probed by our sample to constrain the occurrence rates of circumbinary planets and brown dwarfs.

     Most confirmed planets known by date were detected in high resolution stellar light curves of the space missions CoRoT, Kepler, K2 and TESS. These light curves are searched for candidates by automatic detection pipelines because of the large data amount.      Circumbinary planets (CBPs) are exoplanets that orbit around eclipsing binaries (EB), systems of two close-moving stars. Despite the discovery of over 5,800 exoplanets, only 14 CBPs have been confirmed, largely due to the significant observational challenges they present. The orbital motion of the binary stars induces extreme transit timing variations (TTVs), causing planetary transits to occur days or even weeks earlier or later than expected. Additionally, the stellar eclipses characteristic of EBs overshadow the subtle transit signals of orbiting planets. These factors make traditional exoplanet detection techniques less effective in identifying CBPs. <P />To overcome these obstacles, I developed BLSiNet, a detection pipeline that integrates Box-fitting Least Squares (BLS) with continuum spline-fitting to accurately mask eclipses and detrend stellar flux, preserving potential planetary signals. Building on this, I trained a convolutional neural network (CNN) on a dataset of 100,000 synthetic light curves modeled after the characteristics of known CBPs. The resulting model achieved an accuracy of 97% and successfully recovered 100% of previously confirmed CBPs. Furthermore, the pipeline demonstrated high computational efficiency, capable of identifying Kepler-35b transit signals with a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of 23 in approximately 25 seconds. Leveraging this performance, I conducted a blind search of the entire Kepler EB catalog. This search led to the discovery of two strong CBP candidates. Subsequent analysis of their eclipse timing variations (ETVs) produced sinusoidal O─C (Observed minus Calculated) diagrams, an established signature of gravitational perturbations consistent with third-body companions, providing further support for the validity of these detections. <P />Together, these results suggest that BLSiNet is a promising approach for scalable and efficient detection of CBPs and may significantly expand the known population of these rare and dynamically unique planetary systems.

To increase this number, we used the Phoebe modelling software to begin the modeling process. We used photometric data in the i, g, and r filters from the Zwicky Transient Facility available through their online archive, but no radial velocity data. So, we held the primary mass constant, and limited the secondary mass to a have main-sequence mass-radius function. A final set of parameters has not been obtained for this system, but the partial eclipse does indicate a likely higher inclination range, with the best results being in the low 80s regime. We present and discuss our best-fitting parameter sets.
  2026-02  article   Smallwood, Jeremy L. et al. | The polar debris disc around 99 Herculis: a potential signpost for polar circumbinary planets | https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026MNRAS.545f1982S arXiv:2510.08698
     The nearby binary star system 99 Herculis (99 Her) is host to the only known polar-aligned circumbinary debris disc. We investigate the hypothesis that the narrow structure of this circumbinary disc is sculpted by the gravitational influence of one or more unseen polar circumbinary planets. We first establish the theoretically viable parameter space for a sculpting planet by considering dynamical stability and clearing mechanisms, including the chaotic zone, Hill's radius, diffusion, and polar alignment time-scales. We then use N-body simulations to test three specific architectures: a single planet interior to the disc, a single planet exterior, and a two-planet system bracketing the disc. Our simulations demonstrate that single-planet models are insufficient to reproduce the observed morphology, as they can only truncate one edge of the disc while leaving the other dynamically extended. In contrast, the two-planet shepherding model successfully carves both the inner and outer edges, confining the debris into a narrow, stable polar ring consistent with observations. We conclude that the structure of the 99 Her debris disc is most plausibly explained by the presence of two shepherding, polar circumbinary planets. We present a specific, testable model for this unique system, which elucidates the pivotal role of planetary bodies in sculpting the architecture of debris discs.
