## What ran
50 stars = the 5 known circumbinary hosts (Kepler-16, 38, 64, 453, 1647) + 45 spread across brightness (15 each below magnitude 11, 11 to 12, and 12 and fainter) drawn from stars whose best sector reaches 15 Earth radii or smaller. 474 star-sector groups, 949 light-curve files (QLP 471, TESS-SPOC 277, SPOC 199; 2 QLP files had no safe windows), no failures, about 15 minutes on 8 cores. Every product of every sector was searched on the real curve and on the upside-down curve, with the same detector (checked identical to `detect_v2.find_events` on 33 files), all catalogue periods masked, QLP limited to borrowed good-time windows, events flagged, not deleted. ## Results (VERIFIED unless marked)
- **No excess of real dips over upside-down bumps.** Events at SNR 7 or more per star-sector (best product): real 0.076, upside-down 0.178. The ratio is below 1 at every threshold (0.43 at SNR 7). So the upside-down count is a conservative (high) false-alarm estimate, probably because flares and scattered light make more brightening bumps than dips.
- **Where the events sit:** two thirds of the real events (24 of 36) and 64% of the fake ones are within 0.5 days of a data gap or momentum dump. Flagged events are not thrown away (a real planet can sit there: 2 of the 4 TOI-1338 b crossings did), they go to a lower-priority second tier.
- **The cuts, applied one after another to the 36 real SNR 7 events (and 84 upside-down):** gap/dump flag 12 (30); same-moment burst 7 (21); matched upside-down 7 (21); not at a catalogue eclipse phase (C5) 4 (12); second product agrees at SNR 4 or more, lenient (events with no second product are kept) 4 (9), strict 1 (2).
- **Second-product check (rule C8) in numbers.** 50% of events have a second product. Dips injected in every product at the same time (about 12 times the star's own noise) were found in the product they went into 77% of the time; a second product then showed them at SNR 4 or more in **91%** (93% at 3, 87% at 5). Old QLP raw flux is the weak confirmer (79 to 81% kept; SPOC and TESS-SPOC as confirmers keep 98 to 99%). Upside-down false alarms were confirmed 49% of the time (21 of 43). So the check removes about half the false alarms and loses about 9% of dips that strong. LIKELY for weaker dips: a marginal planet near SNR 7 will lose more; not measured.
- **Eclipse leakage (the check the review asked for).** Real events within 2 eclipse widths of the masked eclipses: 17% of real events against 5% of upside-down ones (6 versus about 2: small numbers, possible leakage). Rule C5 (within 0.35 days of a catalogue primary eclipse or of phase 0.5) catches two survivors that sat on eclipses the automatic mask had missed on variable, noisy stars. C5 removes about the same share of real (17%) and fake (19%) events, so it is a safety rule, not a discriminator, and it costs the part of the timeline near eclipses.
- **Known hosts (negative controls).** Real events: Kepler-16 3, Kepler-38 0, Kepler-64 1, Kepler-453 1, Kepler-1647 2; two passed the flag cuts. One is a 6.6%-deep ramp in old QLP raw flux (junk). The other, on Kepler-453, has about the known planet's depth (0.5%) but is 4 hours long (the planet's Kepler transits last 7 to 14 hours), is confirmed in a second product at SNR 4.6, and sits 0.10 days from phase 0.5, where the binary's secondary eclipse is expected: most likely the binary, not the planet. Both are consistent with chance (about 0.7 unflagged false events expected across the controls).
- **Projection to the whole campaign (rates per brightness class times the campaign's 5,064 star-sectors; based on 4 to 7 events, so uncertain by about a factor of two):** real events about 440 in all; after gap, dump, burst and upside-down flags about 90; also not at an eclipse phase about 60 (upside-down: 105 to 130). The flagged real events (about 350) go to a lower-priority tier. A first-tier list near 60 is small enough for a person; at SNR 7 with no second product it is not a "candidate" list, only a "look at these" list.
- **Survivors (all checks, non-control): 3 events on 3 stars** (strongest SNR 27; 2 at SNR 10 or more; 1 confirmed in a second product). Two have no second product at all (QLP-only stars). A local contact sheet and phase check by Claude (key 1 only): the 5 survivors before C5 were 2 on a primary eclipse that the mask missed, 1 weak step; after C5 the 3 left are 1 weak low-SNR event and 2 clean-looking dips that need a pixel check and a neighbour check before anyone calls them anything. **They are leads, not candidates.** Details stay local.
- **Not tested here:** the duration-versus-binary-phase check (C4), pair searches, pixel checks, and any single-product event on a QLP-only star beyond looking at its shape.

## What it changes
- The vetting load is plausible: about 60 first-tier events campaign-wide, not hundreds. The same-moment filter, the gap/dump flags, the eclipse-phase check and the second-product check each remove a different slice.
- Weak points to fix before or during Stage 3: (1) the automatic eclipse mask misses eclipses on variable noisy stars, so C5 (catalogue phase) should be part of the pipeline; (2) QLP-only stars (237) cannot be confirmed by a second product; (3) the per-sector false-alarm estimate is high by 2x, so use real-curve rates for list-size planning.
- Not credited as "searched": these 50 stars will be searched again in Stage 3 with settings that may change.
