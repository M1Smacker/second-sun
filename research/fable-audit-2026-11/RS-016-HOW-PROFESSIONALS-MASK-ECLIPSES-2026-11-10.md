# RS-016: how do the professionals remove stellar eclipses before looking for circumbinary transits? (10 Nov 2026, Sonnet 5.5 Extra)

**Plain verdict: found something. The two closest published searches (STANLEY and the 2026 TESS single-transit search mono-cbp) do NOT find eclipses in the flux. They mask the in-eclipse cadences from the CATALOGUE ephemeris, period and eclipse widths. That kind of mask depends on time only, so (a) the order of injection and masking cannot matter, (b) the real and upside-down arms get the identical mask by construction. Armstrong 2014 used a data-driven fold, but over about four years of Kepler cycles, so each phase bin held many cycles and the bin median could not contain a planet. Our frozen mask folds ONE sector, where a slow binary visits each bin once.**

## What to copy (comparison with `design/PETROVA-1.0-MASK-DESIGN.md`)
1. **Make the mask a function of time only, from the catalogue** (the design's rule 6, "catalogue window"). It removes the whole problem class: injection order stops mattering, f and 2 minus f get the same mask (the design's rule 8, the mirror rule, becomes unnecessary), and the cost becomes the known, small, countable loss of planet dips that overlap a true eclipse. This is what STANLEY and mono-cbp do.
2. **Why we did not:** Vesper used a data-driven mask because it needed one for 549 Kepler stars and later hunts without trusted widths (UNCERTAIN, my reading of the history). The design note says the catalogue window "removed almost nothing" because the Prsa phase convention (`Phip-pf`, `Phis-pf`) is unverified. So the first job is to verify that convention on a known system and measure ephemeris drift to sector 100.
3. **Armstrong's median subtraction is the principle behind the repeat rule** (an eclipse is what comes back over many cycles). It is sound when many cycles share a bin, and it fails on one sector of a slow binary, exactly Fable's F-01.
4. **Widths and padding:** mono-cbp uses the catalogue widths with no extra pad; Thornton expands to 3.5 times a fitted duration for ingress and egress; ours pads 1.5 times a data-found width. The pad is a tunable that must be chosen on injected dips and synthetic eclipses only, never on real events.
5. **Syzygies:** a catalogue mask loses planet dips that overlap a real eclipse; the lost share is roughly the eclipse duty cycle and can be measured exactly in the Part A harness (inject before the mask, count). It is a limit to state in the book, not a bug.

## Plan changes
- New candidate for the Opus design step, ahead of Amendment A's hybrid: **"catalogue mask" (candidate C0)**, tested first in the Part A harness (it is a time-only function, so the harness runs it by swapping `mask_runs` for a catalogue-window function). Pass marks unchanged: before/after m at least 0.9 in every bin at K 12 and 25; real/upside-down ratio 0.8 to 1.25 (this is automatic for a time-only mask, so the check mostly guards against bugs); no new events inside catalogue eclipse windows.
- If C0 fails, the reason will be informative: phase convention wrong, eccentric secondaries mislocated, or ephemeris drift. The repeat-confirmed hybrid (Amendment A) stays as the fallback for stars without trusted catalogue widths.
- The single-visit lane of the design is still needed for eclipses the catalogue misses, but its job shrinks.
- Questions left open: how large is the loss to syzygies in G2 and G3 for C0 (a number, from the harness); does the TTC catalogue (G3) give widths and secondary phase in the same form as Prsa 2022; are there stars with no usable catalogue widths (count).
