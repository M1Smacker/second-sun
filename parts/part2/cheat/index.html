    Cheat sheet: Part 2, Two suns¶

    The chapters in three lines¶

    4. Two suns

    • What: binary stars, and the planets that orbit both stars at once.
    • Why: only 14 have ever been seen crossing their stars, so each new one matters. VERIFIED
    • You will be able to: explain what a circumbinary planet is and why we hunt them.

    5. Wandering transits

    • What: why a two-sun planet's dips come early, late, longer or shorter.
    • Why: that wandering hides them from normal search tools, which is our chance.
    • You will be able to: say why a search that expects a steady clock misses them.

    6. Orbits and the three-body problem

    • What: Kepler's law, the closest safe orbit, and why three pulling bodies have no simple formula.
    • Why: a computer has to step through the motion, and that is what STANLEY does.
    • You will be able to: read a crossing's time and length as a clue to where the stars were.

    Words to know¶

    • Circumbinary planet: A planet that orbits both stars of a pair.
    • Binary star: Two stars held together by gravity, each circling their shared centre.
    • Kepler: NASA's space telescope that stared at one patch of sky from 2009 to 2013 and found thousands of planets and eclipsing binaries.
    • TESS: NASA's Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite, launched in 2018.
    • Transit: A planet passing in front of its star as seen from Earth, which makes a small dip in the light curve.
    • Precession: The slow turning of a planet's orbit around a pair of stars.
    • SPOC: NASA's Science Processing Operations Center: makes the cleanest TESS light curves, from 2-minute postage stamps.
    • Sector: One TESS stretch of about 27 days looking at the same patch of sky.
    • N-body simulation: A computer calculation that moves several objects (like two stars and a planet) step by step under each other's gravity.
    • STANLEY: A search tool by David Martin's group (Martin et al.

    Numbers to remember¶

    • 14 transiting circumbinary planets are known: 12 from Kepler and 2 from TESS (TOI-1338 b and TIC 172900988 b) VERIFIED
    • Kepler-16 b (2011) was the first transiting circumbinary planet: Saturn-like, about 229-day orbit around a pair of stars on a 41-day orbit LIKELY
    • Holman and Wiegert (1999) give the closest stable circumbinary orbit as a fitted formula in the binary's eccentricity e and mass ratio mu VERIFIED
    • For the known transiting systems the closest stable orbit is 2.5 to 3.5 times the binary separation, i.e. an orbital period 4 to 7 times the binary period VERIFIED
    • The innermost transiting circumbinary planets sit at only 1.3 to 1.6 times the critical period (TOI-1338 b: 1.34) VERIFIED
    • TOI-1338 b orbits its two stars every 95.2 days; it is about 6.9 Earth radii; the stars orbit each other every 14.6 days (eccentricity 0.16, about 1.1 and 0.3 solar masses) VERIFIED
    • Circumbinary transit timing can shift by days, longer than a transit lasts, so folding at one period fails; all Kepler circumbinary discoveries were made by eye VERIFIED
    • TOI-1338 b's first three TESS transits were about 93 and 95 days apart and lasted about 0.3, 0.4 and 0.6 days VERIFIED
