## Chapter 4: Two suns

[Read the chapter](../../book/04-two-suns.md)

??? quiz "1. What's the difference between an S-type and a P-type planet?"
    S-type orbits **one** star of the pair. P-type (circumbinary) orbits **both**. We hunt P-type.

??? quiz "2. A binary has a period of 10 days. Roughly where would you expect its innermost stable planet?"
    The critical period is 4 to 7 times the binary period: **40 to 70 days**. Known planets pile up at 1.3 to 1.6 times that, so roughly **50 to 110 days**.

??? quiz "3. A planet sits at 4 times the stars' separation. About how many binary periods does it take to orbit once?"
    4<sup>3/2</sup> = √(4³) = √64 = **8** binary periods.

## Chapter 5: Wandering transits

[Read the chapter](../../book/05-wandering-transits.md)

??? quiz "1. Why can't we fold a two-sun planet's light curve at one period?"
    Its transit times shift by up to days, so the dips don't line up at any single period.

??? quiz "2. TOI-1338 b's transits lasted 0.3, 0.4 and 0.6 days. What causes the difference?"
    The crossed star's own motion. Moving with the planet makes a long transit; moving against it makes a short one.

??? quiz "3. In one sentence, what is G1?"
    Searching Kepler's eclipsing binaries in newer TESS data for planets that weren't crossing during Kepler but have **switched on** since, because their orbits precessed.

## Chapter 6: Orbits and the three-body problem

[Read the chapter](../../book/06-orbits-three-body.md)

??? quiz "1. A planet orbits a pair of 1-Sun-total stars at 1 AU. How long is its year?"
    *P*² = 1³ / 1 = 1, so **1 year**. Same as Earth around the Sun: Kepler's law only cares about the total mass inside.

??? quiz "2. Why are TOI-1338's two eclipses 0.453 of an orbit apart, not 0.5?"
    The stars' orbit is **eccentric** (stretched). The stars move faster when close together, so the time from one eclipse to the next is shorter one way round.

??? quiz "3. A binary has a critical period of 50 days. Should we search for planets with 30-day orbits?"
    **No.** Planets that close would be unstable and wouldn't survive. Start at 50 days.

??? quiz "4. Why might one crossing last 6 hours and the next 12?"
    The star moves too. When it moves the same way as the planet, they pass each other slowly (long crossing); in opposite directions, quickly (short crossing).
