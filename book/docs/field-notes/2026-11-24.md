# 24 November 2026: the lists run out, the light does not

<p class="ss-meta">Field note · day 37 · about 3 min</p>

!!! plain "In plain words"
    We put every public list of eclipsing binaries together and counted what is left for us. The answer: very few long-period pairs that we have not searched already. The big pool is a list of 10,531 newly found bright pairs, and most of them have no known period at all. So the Census's next step is to find periods ourselves, from TESS light.

## The lists, merged

| List | Pairs | What it gives |
|---|---|---|
| Prsa et al. 2022 (TESS) | 4,584 | period, eclipse time, eclipse widths |
| TESS Ten Thousand, new pairs | 7,936 | period, eclipse time, eclipse lengths in hours |
| TESS Ten Thousand, known pairs | 2,065 | period and eclipse time only |
| CALNet | 10,531 | which stars are pairs; a Gaia period for 1,559 |

Together: 24,902 stars. A check that could have failed: our reading of the rule that picked the G2 hunt list rebuilt that list exactly, 895 of 895 stars.

| What is left | Stars |

**The lesson:** the catalogues were built for other jobs, and the slow pairs a planet hunt wants are exactly the ones they time worst (yesterday: Gaia's periods for slow pairs are mostly wrong). The new work is a period finder for one star's TESS light, checked first on 150 pairs whose answers are known. It is written for Codex, with its pass marks fixed before it runs.
