# 10 November 2026: our eclipse mask was deleting planets, and our tests could not see it

<p class="ss-meta">Field note · day 23 · about 8 min</p>

!!! plain "In plain words"
    An outside audit found a flaw in how we tested our own search. The step that hides the stars' eclipses can also hide a planet's dip, and every fake-planet test we ran skipped that step's damage. When we hid the fakes the way a real planet would be hidden, the old search lost 4 to 6 of every 10 strong dips. Our searches still show no excess of real dips over noise. What they rule out is much less than we wrote.

## What we found

1. **The mask.** Before searching, the old search cuts out the eclipses. It finds them by folding one sector of light on the binary's period and cutting the parts of the cycle that are low. In a slow binary each part of the cycle is seen once per sector, so a planet's dip looks like an eclipse and is cut with them.
2. **Our tests skipped it.** The fake-planet tests either built the mask from clean light, or added the fake dip after the mask. A real planet is in the light when the mask is built. So the tests never saw the loss.
3. **Measured the right way.** We put fake dips into real light before the mask and counted what survived (1,774 sampled files from the second and third star lists, 1,660 usable, one fake per file and size). In the second list, strong dips (25 times the noise, 8 hours long) survived like this:

| Binary period | 5 to 10 d | 10 to 14 d | 14 to 20 d | 20 to 27 d | over 27 d |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Found, fake added after the mask (old test) | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.99 |
| Found, fake added before the mask (like a real planet) | 0.66 | 0.26 | 0.07 | 0.01 | 0.02 |

4. **Our own planet star.** On TOI-1338's 40 sectors, fakes of the three larger sizes together were found 0.58 of the time before the mask against 0.95 after. A 25-times-noise, 8-hour dip: 0.28 against 1.00.
5. **A published planet.** In one data set of TIC 172900988 b, both of its published dips are deleted by the old mask.
6. **Mirror bumps are not lost.** The upside-down copy of the light is a fair control only if both versions are treated alike. The old mask cuts dips and not bumps, so the control was unfair in slow binaries.
7. **We broke our own test on purpose** to check it can fail. It did, each time.

## What the professionals do

The two closest published searches (STANLEY, and a 2026 search for single transits in TESS binaries) do not guess the eclipses from the light. They read each binary's timetable from a catalogue: period, time of the eclipse, how long it lasts, and cut only those hours. That does not depend on the planet, so a planet cannot be mistaken for an eclipse.

## What we tried

- **Catalogue timetable only.** Planted dips survive (98 to 100 out of 100 in both lists). But eclipses missing from the timetable leak through, and only the real light shows them: about twice as many real dips as upside-down ones, so the control is no longer fair.
- **Timetable plus "a dip that comes back at the same point of the cycle is an eclipse".** Planted dips survive (99 out of 100), and the control is fair again for binaries under about 13 days. Slow binaries still leak. A separate step that removes recurring events across sectors is being tested.

## What this changes

- Every "found X percent", "smallest planet" and "floor of 6 Earth radii" number in this book was measured the old way. Read it as an upper limit. Each page where it appears now carries a correction.
- **Not changed:** our three searches found no excess of real dips over noise. That still stands.
- **What the nulls can exclude:** much less than we wrote, above all for binaries slower than about 14 days.
- **Next:** finish the new mask, freeze it as a new version, and search the light we already have again. Real events stay private.

## Check yourself

3. Why is the upside-down light no longer a fair control with the old mask? (It cuts dips but leaves bumps.)
