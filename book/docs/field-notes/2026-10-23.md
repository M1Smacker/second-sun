## The idea behind it

A map is a promise about where things are. To draw part of the sky on a flat page you need each star's **position** (right ascension and declination, like longitude and latitude on the sky) and a rule for flattening a sphere. We used a **gnomonic projection**, which keeps straight lines straight and distorts only a little over a patch this size. Brightness becomes dot size: a smaller **magnitude** number means a brighter star, so it gets a bigger dot. Colour comes from the star's spectral type. That is why the map is real: change the catalogue and the picture changes.

## How you could do it yourself

Open `book/data/path.yml`: it lists the Parts, the fifteen chapters, their summaries and the journey order. Edit a line, then run `.venv-docs\Scripts\python.exe -m mkdocs build --strict` inside `book`, and the map, the strips and the Next links all follow. To redraw the sky from fresh catalogues, `book/tools/make_sky.py` (needs the internet) rewrites `book/data/sky.json`.

## Later the same night: breaking it on purpose

After the build I tried to break the book, which is where the real lessons were.

- **A tool crashed on our map.** The theme rewrites every link on a page when you move between pages, and that code throws an error on links *inside* an SVG picture, because an SVG link's address is read-only. A screenshot would never show it; a browser test that listens for errors did. The map's links now use the older `xlink:href` form, and the error is gone.
- **Sizes need a ruler, not an eye.** A script measured every tappable thing at phone width. The map's dots were about 15 pixels wide, too small for a thumb, and several links were short. They are now at least 24 pixels (the map) or 44 (everything else), and the check runs before every publish.
- **Numbers must agree on a page.** Building the explorer from the real data showed that Chapter 2 gave the primary eclipse as 5.1% and the secondary as 0.4%, while our measured values (claim F-046) are about 6% at the bottom (5.7% typical) and 0.3%. The table now uses the measured values with their label.
- **Facts need their labels even in summaries.** The new summaries repeated numbers (14 planets, one in four candidates) without the little VERIFIED chips. They have them now.
- **A rule only counts if you have seen it refuse.** Ten deliberately broken copies (a missing chapter, a sidebar that disagrees with the page order, a story that is too long) are each refused by `book/tools/test_shape.py`. A page nobody links to, a stray dash and a colour that fails 7-to-1 are also caught.

**Idea:** test the failure, not the happy path. A check you have never seen fail is a hope, not a check.

**You could try it:** in `book/data/path.yml`, change one chapter's `part` and run the build. It stops with a plain message instead of publishing a book whose map and menu disagree.

## Questions

??? quiz "1. Why do bigger dots mean brighter stars on the map?"
    Magnitude is backwards: a smaller number is brighter. The map turns that into a dot size, so the brightest stars (like Deneb) are drawn largest.

??? quiz "2. Why does the caption say 546 drawn, not 549?"
    Three of our stars have no sky position in the Villanova Kepler catalogue, and we only draw what we can place. The book says so rather than rounding up.
