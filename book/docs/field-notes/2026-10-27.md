## What we did, and why

1. **The design brief was checked with a calculator.** Another AI wrote a brief for a new look. I ran it through Compass (my review step). Its "muted brass" text colour measured 2.9 to 1 against our rule of 7 to 1. The brief asked for 60 to 70 characters per line, but a phone at 375 pixels fits only about 35 to 42. The revised brief then failed its own colour rule on two surfaces (2.94 and 2.69 against a minimum of 3). A script found that, not reading.
2. **The font report was checked against the font files.** You do not like the current look, so we asked another AI which fonts would suit the book. Its answer was sensible, but it never said which characters each font has. I read the character list inside the four font files the book serves today. All four lack σ, μ, λ, Δ, θ, ≈, ≥, ≤ and →. The book uses σ 38 times, ≈ 28 times and μ 11 times, so on your phone those characters come from the phone's own font, not from Inter. That may be part of why the type feels off.
3. **A font test you can read on your phone.** A private page shows a real paragraph of this book in five font pairings, night or day, and checks which symbols each font has. Nothing in the book changes until you choose.
4. **The learning path was reviewed before it was built.** The book has three reading orders: chapter numbers, the order the project needed them, and the seven learning levels. Four chapters (3, 13, 14, 15) belong to no level at all. The review recommends one path (the levels), keeps chapter numbers as permanent labels, and gives the orphan chapters a home. You decide before anything is built.
5. **Eyes mode now says your score first.** After every practice or test, the top of the finish screen says "You got N of 30 right", then how many were wrong and Not sure, and how often you picked each answer. You no longer open My results to find it.
6. **Two pages for professional readers.** *For astronomers* says in one page what is solid and what is not, and *Prior work* lists the published searches we know and how deeply we have read each. Both say plainly that we have found no planet.

## The idea behind it

**Idea (measure the claim):** advice is a claim. Some claims have a number that can contradict them, such as a contrast ratio, a character count or a list of characters in a file. For those, checking with a tool beats reading, because a tool can say no. A **contrast ratio** compares the brightness of text and its background; 7 to 1 means the lighter is seven times brighter once a small allowance is added to both. Black on white is 21 to 1. A **character map** is the list of characters a font file can draw; if a character is missing, the device quietly borrows one from another font.

**Idea (an honest label on every claim):** the font report marked some facts as verified and some as estimates, and the estimates were true estimates. That is the standard we hold ourselves to: say which is which.

**Idea (decide before you build):** the learning-path review ended with three questions for you, not with code. Changing the order of a book changes how you learn, and that is your call.
