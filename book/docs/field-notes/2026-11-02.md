# 2 November 2026: before judging a change, make sure you are comparing like with like

<p class="ss-meta">Field note · day 15 · about 7 min</p>

!!! plain "In plain words"
    We built the eye test that decides when you may look at real candidates, and we tested a possible improvement to the search. While testing the improvement we nearly compared a new run with an old run that had been made under different conditions. A consistency check stopped us before any verdict was read. We reran the old setting under today's conditions instead.

## What we did, and why

1. **The rehearsal (step three of the ladder to real data).** Eight sessions of 20 items, in the same three-panel layout the real candidates will use. Every item is real TESS light with at most one thing hidden in it: a fake planet (clear or near the limit), a real eclipse, an instrument effect, a flare, a step, or nothing. You answer, lock, and only then see the truth. The pass line was written before the page existed.
2. **Testing it three ways before it went live.** Unit checks of the arithmetic (38), a browser test at three screen sizes (73 checks), and a test that plays a whole session as your device against a copy of the cloud (12 checks). We also posted one real rehearsal round to the real cloud rules and deleted it at once, to be sure a refusal could not silently leave your answers waiting on your phone.
3. **Keeping the scores apart.** Rehearsal rounds are counted on their own, so they never mix into your practice numbers.
4. **A change to the search, judged by rules written first.** Three small switches (a depth limit, a fix for one broken light curve, a flare-shape check) were run on the development stars only. The accept rules were written in a plan before any run.
5. **A comparison that was not like for like.** The plan assumed an older run of the development stars held the same events as a run today. Our own analysis script checked this first and refused to continue: 77 of 583 groups of one kind had different events.

## The idea behind it

A comparison only means something if the two sides differ in one thing. Here a second thing had changed. Stars that have no data of their own in one product borrow good stretches of data from neighbouring stars in the same sector. Between the two runs we had downloaded more stars, so the list of neighbours had grown. Nothing was wrong with either run; they were just not comparable. See [Chapter 15](../book/15-mistakes-as-lessons.md) and the new check at the end of the [learning track](../learn/index.md).

## What we found, and what we did not

- **Found:** the cause of the difference (CONFIRMED: every one of the 77 changed groups borrows its windows, none of the 2,651 groups with their own windows changed).
- **Found:** a rehearsal round is accepted by the cloud rules, cannot be changed or deleted from outside, and is read back with all 20 answers.
- **Study result:** under the rules written first, all three switches pass on the development stars. The depth limit and the fix for the broken light curve change nothing there (they are safety guards). The flare-shape check at its most aggressive setting removes 11 of 22 false alarms (half, but only 22 of them, on 18 stars) at the cost of about 1 percentage point of the planted fake planets it recovers (38% down to about 37%). This is a development result, and nothing is frozen: the fresh star list is the clean test.
- **Not tested:** Safari on a real iPhone and real touch gestures for the new page (only a simulated phone was used).
- **Not decided:** whether the borrowing should be frozen per star list; this is logged as a bug (item 70) to decide before the next star list is searched.

## Questions

1. Why did the study stop to rerun the old setting instead of using the old run?
2. Why were the accept rules written before the runs?
3. Why is a good result in the rehearsal necessary but not enough?

Answers: 1. The old run had been made with a smaller list of neighbouring stars, so a difference between the runs could not be blamed on the new switches alone. 2. Rules written after seeing results can be bent to fit them. 3. The fake planets are simple shapes planted in real light; a real planet may look a little different.
