# 11 November 2026: we searched the hidden light again, found nothing, and learned how to read that

<p class="ss-meta">Field note · day 24 · about 8 min</p>

!!! plain "In plain words"
    We searched the light our old filter used to throw away, in 803 binary stars that were never used to design anything. The real dips did not beat the upside-down control. Nothing to check and no leads. The most useful part came before the search: our own test would have shouted "excess" even with no planet in the sky, and we caught it in time.

## What we found

1. **The new filter keeps the light.** It is frozen as Petrova 1.0.0 (the new name for Vesper). Switched off, it gives exactly the old answers (300 of 300 test star-sectors, three ways). Planted fake dips and fake bumps survive it as well as they survive without it.
2. **The old test would have lied.** In the light the old filter deleted, which sits next to the binary's own eclipses, real dips outnumber upside-down dips 2 to 4 times even after the new mask (on the design stars: 33 against 8 in one list, 65 against 33 in the other). In light both filters keep, the two sides are balanced (10 against 13). The likely cause is the leftover edges of the eclipses: they only dip, they never bump, so they only show on the real side.
3. **So we fixed the rule first, in writing.** The new stars are compared with the design stars, bin by bin of binary period, and the rule was committed before any new star was read.
4. **The answer.** New stars, real against upside-down strong dips in the recovered light: 16 against 6 in the third list, 34 against 13 in the second. That is the same imbalance as on the design stars. Chance of seeing this if nothing new is there: 0.41 and 0.31. The mark was 0.005. No excess.
5. **How big a miss would we have noticed?** Only an excess of about 5 times (third list) and about 3.3 to 3.7 times (second list) the expected count. (Corrected 13 Nov 2026: the first figure for the second list, 2.7, came from a simpler test than the one that gave the verdict; an outside re-check with the right test gave 3.3 to 3.7.) About 4 in 10 planted strong dips survive the whole chain, and that is measured only in light the mask kept.
6. **Where the stars are.** Of the 803 new stars, only 91 rows are slow binaries (14 days or more). The other 335 slow binaries that were never used for design are in the Kepler list (G1), which has no eclipse widths yet and was not searched.

## What this changes

- Our fourth search is a clean null for binaries of 5 to 14 days in the light the old filter deleted, with the limits above. For slow binaries the question is still open.
- A null has a size. Always read the "how big an excess would show" line beside it.
- The control is only fair if both sides are treated alike. Near eclipses they were not, and the fix was to compare like with like.
- **Next:** get the eclipse widths for the Kepler list and search it; then a filter that knows the binary phase, so the leftover edges cannot pass as dips.

## Check yourself

1. Why must a control treat both sides alike? (Otherwise a difference between them can come from the control, not from the sky.)
2. Why compare new stars with design stars instead of using a formula? (The imbalance is real and has no formula; the design stars measure it.)
3. Why does "no excess" not mean "no planets"? (Only an excess above the power line would have been seen, and the completeness is about 4 in 10.)
4. Why does it matter where the slow binaries are? (The old filter hurt them most, and most of the unseen ones are in a list we did not search.)
