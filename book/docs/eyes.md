# Eyes mode

<p class="ss-meta">Hunt · practice and the official test · every answer saved</p>

!!! quote "The story"
    In the first six quarters of Kepler data, volunteers on a website called Planet Hunters found a planet by looking at light curves by eye. It crosses the larger of two stars, which circle each other every 20 days, about once every 137 days, and it was named PH1 b {{claim:F-059}}. A second pair of stars, about 1,000 times the Earth-Sun distance away, makes it the first transiting planet known in a four-star system {{claim:F-059}}. Ahmed, that is why this book has an eyes mode: some planets have been found by people looking.

!!! plain "In plain words"
    You get a short stretch of real light curve with nothing marked on it. Say Dip, No dip, Odd or Not sure. After every answer a big banner says what it truly was: a dip, or not a dip and why. Every answer is saved on your device, so your scores are kept. It is practice and a test: the dips are fake planets we hid, plus glitches and real eclipses that we know about.

## How it works

- **Blind first.** Nothing is marked until you answer, then the hidden thing is outlined.
- **Four answers.** *Dip* is a planet-like dip that comes back up. *No dip* means nothing there. *Odd* is something that is not a planet: an eclipse, a spike or a sudden jump. *Not sure* is always allowed, and it is honest.
- **The banner tells you the truth.** After each answer in practice it says **THIS WAS: A DIP** or **THIS WAS: NOT A DIP**, what it was, and whether you were right. If you said *No dip* to an eclipse, it says you were right that it is not a dip, but that it was an eclipse.
- **What the numbers say.** In practice, and in the review after a test, three short lines are worked out from the dots of that very curve: how strong its best dip is on the hunt's own scale (7 or more is what the hunt keeps), how it compares with the next best wiggle, whether it ends at a hole in the data, whether the level jumps and stays, and so on. Every number in them was measured on that curve, the three lines are always about the same length, and the wording rotates so you do not read the same sentence twice. The lines pick what to say from the measurements alone, never from the hidden answer. The answer only decides the last line: what it was, and what to check next time. When a curve comes close to a known artifact pattern without crossing its line, the lines say it is not classified instead of calling it plain noise.
- **A line on the plot.** After you answer in practice, a dashed line shows how deep a dip must be, over the best stretch of that curve, to score 7. A solid line shows how deep that stretch really is. You can see at a glance whether it reaches the hunt's bar.
- **A miss comes back.** In practice, when you get a curve wrong, a curve of the same kind is moved to 3 to 5 curves later (the sitting keeps exactly the same curves). Tests are never reordered.
- **Your habit by strength.** *My results* shows, for the curves measured since this was added, how often you said Dip at each strength (under 3, 3 to 5, 5 to 7, 7 to 10, 10 or more), the strength from which you say Dip on more than half of the curves, and your d-prime and criterion. The hunt's line is 7, so the table shows whether your own line is higher or lower.
- **Look back, read only.** Press *Look at previous question* (or the left arrow) to see an earlier curve and what you answered. You can never change an answer.
- **Keys.** On a keyboard, press 1 to 4 to answer and Enter for the next one. Left and right arrows look back and forward, Escape returns.
- **Practice sittings** of 5, 20, 50 or 100 light curves show the answer after every curve.
- **The official test** is 30 curves (10 clear dips, 4 faint ones, 8 quiet, 8 odd things) with no answers until the end. It counts towards the pass line: at least 85% of the clear dips found and at most 1 in 10 quiet curves called a dip, over at least 60 curves, in at least 2 rounds, on at least 2 different days. The faintest dips are reported but are not part of the line. Claude decides, from your results, when you are ready.
- **The HARD test** (step two of the ladder to real data) is 30 curves with its own line, kept apart from the official one. Its cards are harder on purpose. The "nothing hidden" cards are not random stretches: each is the best-looking dip found in a whole sector of fresh noise made from this star's own noise, which is what the hunt hands you. Half of them are shaped like a planet's dip (as long as a fake planet), the other half are the natural kind, because real noise peaks are short. Its fake planets are 3 to 12 hours long and go down to the edge of what anyone can see. Strength is the hunt's own box score, measured on the finished curve (about 10 to 14 for the clear ones, 4 to 9 for the near-limit ones; the hunt keeps 7 and above). Hard noise scores about 4.5 in the middle and roughly 1 curve in 6 reaches 7 or more. The odd things include dips that end at a gap in the data (the classic thruster artifact), jumps down that never recover, and subtle spikes. The mix is 6 clear fake planets, 6 near the limit, 8 hard-noise cards and 10 odd things. *Not sure* is a fair answer on the hardest cards and is free there, but on a clear fake planet it counts as a miss. The line, fixed before it was first played: at least 80% of the clear fake planets found and at most 20% of the hard-noise cards called a dip, over at least 4 hard rounds, 120 curves and 2 different days. *Hard practice* shows the answer after every curve.
- **Your results are saved after every answer**, on this device, and a round you leave half done is kept as abandoned, then sent to the cloud like the others. The *My results* button shows every round: right, wrong, not sure, dips found and false alarms. You never have to send anything to Claude: the moment you finish a round (or leave one half done), it is sent by itself to a private cloud copy. If you were offline, it waits on your phone and goes the moment the internet is back. A line under the results says so ("all 3 finished rounds are safe in the cloud, and Claude can read them"). Add this book to your phone's Home Screen so the phone keeps your rounds while they wait.
- **Practice only for now.** Every stretch comes from the real TOI-1338 light curve (TESS sector 98) with answers we know. The real candidates of the hunt get their own tab here once Claude agrees your results are good enough.
