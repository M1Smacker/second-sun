    14. Credit and publishing¶

    What this chapter answers: once we find something, how does it become a recognised discovery with your name on it?

    In plain words

    Ahmed, a dip doesn't become "ours" when we see it. It becomes ours when it's written up and published, and then shared with the TESS team. In 2026 the rules changed: the public candidate site now wants the write-up published first. This chapter is the sample page for the book's chapter layout.

    You need first: L6 Two-key vetting (outline).

    The order of steps¶

    1. Two keys: my analysis and your own check both pass.
    2. Write-up: a short note in RNAAS (free), or a full paper with a professional team.
    3. ExoFOP: only a published candidate can be uploaded, and uploading needs approval VERIFIED. This rule arrived on 19 August 2026, after a pause that started on 31 March VERIFIED.
    4. TESS team: they decide whether it becomes a TOI.

    Journals and AI¶

    • MNRAS: AI can't be an author, and how we used it (including code and data processing) goes in the cover letter and in the paper VERIFIED. You can submit without a university VERIFIED.
    • A&A: AI can't be an author; help with language, coding and searching needs no disclosure if a person oversees it LIKELY.

    What this means for our hunt¶

    Plan the write-up as part of vetting, not after it. The first candidate's timeline is: two keys, RNAAS draft, publication, then ExoFOP.

    Open questions¶

    • Does citing published tools we use count as "a previously published method" for an RNAAS route? Only ExoFOP support can say.
