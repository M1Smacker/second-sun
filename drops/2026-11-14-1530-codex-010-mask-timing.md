**Plain:** Narrowing the eclipse mask's timing padding catches about 25 more test planets and lifts the score past the 50% bar, but lets more noise through on the stars it affects.
**Why it matters:** it is the biggest single fixable loss we know, and the first change that crosses the G1 bar on its own.
**Explanation:** The mask hides eclipses with a window widened by how unsure the eclipse timing is. On 112 G2 stars with old timings that padding is so wide it blanks whole light curves. Turning the padding off (V1) or recentring the windows on the real eclipses with a cap (V2) gives the planets back, but the same stars then show more events on real light and a few more upside-down alarms.
**Technical:** P (equal-bin dip recovery, 1,098 planets) V0 48.37% (528), V1 50.68% (553), V2 50.54% (551); bumps 486, 501, 503. Affected-star background real/upside-down V0 61/3, V1 95/5, V2 90/8. Paired dip-vs-bump 99% intervals fail in 20 to 27.4 d and 27.4+ d for all three, including V0, so that failure is the pre-OAE baseline, not the variants. Eclipse-overlap proxy: no new real-event centres inside eclipse windows (approximate for missing secondaries). RS-025 cause counts reproduced exactly (690 found, 408 not). Not adoptable yet: re-run with OAE on, and explain the 29 to 34 new real-light events.
