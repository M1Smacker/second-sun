    24 October 2026: feedback that is measured, not copied¶

    Field note · day 6 · about 9 min

    In plain words

    You said the comments under each card felt copy-pasted. They were: a few hand-written sentences per kind of noise. Now every card gets three lines worked out from its own dots, in practice and in the review after a test. Before building that, we found that our own practice cards gave their answer away in a way you could learn without learning the sky, and fixed most of it.

    What we did, and why¶

    1. A review before any building. Claude AI wrote a report and a working prototype for the feedback lines. I ran it through Compass (my review step), tested it on our cards and on your exact hard-practice cards, and the verdict was revise: good idea, five things to fix first. Nothing was built until you chose to.
    2. One strength score everywhere. The card banner and the new lines were measuring "strength" in two different ways, so one of your hard-noise cards read 7.3 in one place and 10.2 in the other. I ported the hunt's own scoring (the box_scores function in the detector) to the page, and a test feeds the same numbers to the Python and to the page and demands the same answers.
    3. The cards stopped giving themselves away (mostly). On the first version the quiet "best dip of a sector" was short (about 3 hours at its best box) while the fake planets were about 6.5 hours, so a quiet card could be told from a fake by its length alone. Now half of the quiet cards are chosen to be as long as a fake planet, the fakes run from 3 to 12 hours, and the test measures how much each feature separates the two kinds.
    4. Three lines, worked out from the dots. Each is 36 to 44 words. Line 1 is a measurement ("the dip ends only 1 hour before a hole in the data"), line 2 says what that usually means, line 3 says what it was and what to check next time. About 20 kinds of fact are measured, each with several wordings that rotate.
    5. The lead lesson never looks at the hidden answer. The answer only decides the last line. Real work will not have an answer to look up, so the practice has to learn to use the dots.
    6. The strength of every card you answer is now saved with your answer. That is the data for a line that shows your own habit, for example how often you said Dip on cards under 7.
    7. Three extras for your practice. After you answer, the plot shows a dashed line (how deep a dip must be here to score 7) and a solid line (how deep the best stretch really is). My results has a new table, Your habit by strength: how often you said Dip at each strength, where you start saying Dip more than half the time, your d-prime and your criterion. And in practice a curve you got wrong comes back 3 to 5 curves later (the sitting keeps the same curves; tests are never reordered).
    8. Twin drill. In Hard mode, two curves with the same noise, one with a faint planet and one without. You pick which. It is never saved and counts towards nothing, because it is practice, not evidence. The cards are built from one seed with and without the planet, and a test checks that outside the planet the two curves are identical.
    9. Broken on purpose 18 ways, every break caught. That includes a lead that peeks at the answer, "missed" said about a false alarm, wrong lengths, a changed scale, a quiet card always placed in the same spot, a criterion with the wrong sign and a miss that comes back too soon.

    The idea behind it¶

    Idea (a leak): if a feature of your practice material predicts the answer but has nothing to do with the sky, you can learn the feature and feel clever. The check is a number between 0 and 1 called AUC: 0.5 means the feature tells the two kinds apart no better than a coin, 1 means it always does. In the first version, length separated faint fake planets from quiet cards at 0.84. Now it is 0.66. Position of the event (0.47), holes in the data (0.46) and how strong the runner-up wiggle is (0.40) are near a coin.

    Idea (honest leftovers): not all of that is a flaw. Noise peaks really are shorter than planet dips, so some separation by length is true to the sky. And strength now separates them at 0.71 (it was 0.50 only because the old cards were matched through the length leak). The lines say this plainly instead of hiding it.

    Idea (blind lead): a text that picks its lesson by looking at the hidden answer is just reading the answer back to you. The lead is chosen from measurements only. I measured how often the answer-aware choice would have led differently: 16% of practice cards.

    Idea (near miss): a card that comes close to a known artifact pattern without crossing its test line is called not classified, never plain noise. On ordinary cards this fires about 3 times in 100. In real mode, 0.8% of artifact cards ended up with "pure noise" as the lead.

    How you could do it yourself¶

    • Run node book/tools/test_eyes_feedback.mjs and read the 14 checks. Change a lesson's test in book/docs/assets/eyes-feedback.js and see which check fails.
    • Run node book/tools/test_eyes_mutations.mjs: it breaks the code 14 ways and prints "caught" for each. A test that still passes on broken code is not testing anything.
    • Open research/compass-reviews/2026-10-24-feedback-engine.md to see the five fixes and the numbers behind them.

    What we found, and what we did not¶

    • Found: the pre-hole sag is caught on 95% of fake gap dips and on 0 of 640 ordinary cards. A brightening is caught on 94% of spikes (0.2% false), eclipses on 100%, level jumps on 72% (1.1% false). Your four missed gap dips would now have read, for example: "In the 2 hours before the hole at 10.5 hours, light sags by 1,100 ppm. A sag that ends exactly where data stops is the classic thruster firing fake."
    • Variety: over 60 hard-noise cards in one sitting there are 28 to 38 different second lines (the prototype gave 20), with the numbers masked. You will still meet a repeat now and then.
    • Faint fakes and hard noise still overlap. Hard noise scores about 4.5 in the middle and about 1 in 6 cards reaches 7. The near-limit fakes measure 4 to 9 once the noise is added. So Not sure stays a fair answer on the faintest ones.
    • Not found out: the hunt's scale here comes from this star's reshuffled noise, which is nearly white beyond a few hours. The real sector is lumpier, so long boxes would score lower on real data than here. The easy sitting's windows are raw, not flattened as the hunt flattens them, so its numbers are a little rougher. And none of this has been tried on a real iPhone yet.

    Questions¶

    1. Why must the lead lesson not look at the hidden answer?

    Because in real work there is no hidden answer. A practice text that quotes the answer teaches you to read the answer, not the dots.

    2. What does an AUC of 0.5 mean for a feature like position?

    It tells the two kinds of card apart no better than a coin toss, so learning it teaches you nothing about the generator.

    3. Why is a card that nearly matches an artifact never called plain noise?

    A near miss means something is there that the tests did not explain. Calling it plain noise would reassure you wrongly, so it is called not classified.
